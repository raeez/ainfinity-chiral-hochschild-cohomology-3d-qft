\section*{I.\quad Two directions}

\noindent
A chiral algebra is a point in the derived symplectic category: a Lagrangian
$\cL_\cA \hookrightarrow \cM_{\mathrm{vac}}(\cA)$. The bar complex is the
endomorphism algebra of this point. The Swiss-cheese operad is the local
composition law. Koszul duality is the symplectic complement. The genus tower
is the deformation of the point over the moduli of curves. The boundary point
determines the shifted-cotangent formal local model; the full ambient requires
the Darboux datum. The entire content of both volumes is the local geometry of
the derived symplectic category at this single point.

The Heisenberg algebra is the simplest instance: every entry can be computed by hand. We begin there, then build the general machine.

\medskip
\noindent
Two operators $a(z_1, t_1)$ and $b(z_2, t_2)$ on $\C \times \R$
interact in two independent directions.

In the \emph{holomorphic direction} ($z_1 \to z_2$), collision produces the OPE residue~$a_{(n)}b$. Volume~I built the entire modular Koszul theory from this mechanism: the bar differential
\[
d_{\barB}[s^{-1}a \,|\, s^{-1}b]
\;=\;
\Res_{z_1 = z_2}\!\bigl[
 a(z_1)\,b(z_2)\cdot\eta_{12}
\bigr],
\qquad
\eta_{12} = d\log(z_1 - z_2),
\]
the Arnold relation ($\eta_{12} \wedge \eta_{23} + \eta_{23} \wedge \eta_{31} + \eta_{31} \wedge \eta_{12} = 0$) forcing $D_\cA^2 = 0$ at genus~$0$, the genus-one curvature $d_{\mathrm{fib}}^2 = \kappa(\cA) \cdot \omega_1$, the conditional curved-Dunn continuation to $g \ge 2$, and the universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_\cA = D_\cA - d_0$ whose projections are the five theorems.

In the \emph{topological direction}, the operators separate: cut $\R$ at a point $t_*$ between $t_1$ and $t_2$. The observables on each half-line depend only on their own operators. The \emph{deconcatenation coproduct} records this separation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preface2-delta}
\Delta[s^{-1}a_1|\cdots|s^{-1}a_n]
\;=\;
\sum_{i=0}^{n}
[s^{-1}a_1|\cdots|s^{-1}a_i]
\;\otimes\;
[s^{-1}a_{i+1}|\cdots|s^{-1}a_n].
\end{equation}
The two directions are independent:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preface2-coderivation}
\Delta \circ D_\cA
\;=\;
(D_\cA \otimes \id \;+\; \id \otimes D_\cA) \circ \Delta.
\end{equation}
The differential is a coderivation of the coproduct. Collisions in $\C$ commute with cuts in $\R$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The bar coalgebra and its three projections.}\enspace
The E$_1$ bar complex $\barB(\cA)$ on $\FM_k(\C) \times \Conf_k^{<}(\R)$ is one coalgebra; three functors extract three objects: cobar $\Omega(\barBch(\cA)) \simeq \cA$ recovers the original algebra (bar-cobar inversion), Verdier duality $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{Ran}}(\barBch(\cA)) \simeq \barBch(\cA^!)$ produces the line-side Koszul dual, and chiral Hochschild cochains $C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA)$ compute the bulk observables. The $\SCchtop$~structure is carried by the chiral derived center: the pair $(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA),\,\cA)$ is the bulk-acting-on-boundary datum. Every chapter of this volume is a different projection of the bar coalgebra.

The projections of $\Theta_\cA$ through the Swiss-cheese window include the celestial OPE hierarchy (soft order~$p$ corresponds to shadow degree $r = p+2$; Remark~\ref{rem:celestial-ope-shadow-degree}) and the conjectural Page-curve package for black hole evaporation. In the raw gravitational transseries model of \S\ref{subsec:gravity-page-curve}, the scalar identity $\kappa + \kappa' = 13$ only normalises the two Virasoro sectors; the Page time $t_P = 3S_{\mathrm{BH}}/13$ additionally requires the two-sector, Stokes, and entropy-extraction hypotheses.

\medskip

The independence is geometric. The bar complex is a formal Koszul
model of the derived fibre product
$\cL \times_{\mathscr{M}}^h \cL$.  In a $(-2)$-shifted symplectic
ambient, a Lagrangian intersection is $(-3)$-shifted; the
$T^*[-1]\cL_\cA$ model belongs to the separate smooth commutative HKR
row, where Hochschild cochains identify with polyvectors.  The
holomorphic direction is the Koszul differential of the Lagrangian
embedding; the topological direction is the groupoid diagonal. The
coderivation property says the diagonal is a groupoid morphism. Both
structures descend from the single fact that the bar complex computes
a derived fiber product. The chiral algebra fixes the formal local
bulk model; the actual ambient stack requires the Darboux $1$-form
(Theorem~\ref{thm:holographic-reconstruction}).

The open-colour dual $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ inherits an algebra structure from the topological direction (deconcatenation dualises to concatenation), and on the chirally Koszul locus this algebra is a dg-shifted Yangian: an $\Ainf$ algebra with spectral $R$-matrix, twisted coproduct, and Yang--Baxter equation (the last being $d^2 = 0$ on the degree-$3$ ordered bar complex). Strictification of the quasi-factorisation package to a spectral factorisation quantum group is governed by the root-sector scalar obstruction classes
$[\epsilon_{\alpha,n}]\in
\mathscr K_n/\operatorname{im}(\delta_{\alpha,n})$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-strictification}. Root multiplicity one
scalarises finite root sectors; it does not kill the obstruction. The
Dynkin coefficient $1/n=\int_0^1(1-t)^{n-1}\,dt$ proves vanishing for
path sectors by the $n$-gon rigidity calculation. Finite non-path
sectors strictify exactly on the scalar-vanishing locus; affine mixed
real--imaginary sectors remain conjectural.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Vol~I engine.}\enspace
Volume~I is Koszul duality on a curve: the bar construction for chiral algebras on an algebraic curve~$X$, with the five main theorems formulated uniformly in genus. Three structure theorems organise the representation-theoretic content. \emph{Algebraicity}: $H(t)^2 = t^4 Q_L(t)$ for a computable quadratic~$Q_L$, so the shadow generating function is algebraic of degree~$2$. \emph{Formality identification}: the shadow obstruction tower agrees with the $L_\infty$ formality obstruction tower at all degrees (Theorem~\ref{V1-thm:shadow-formality-identification}), and the discriminant $\Delta = 8\kappa S_4$ classifies algebras into four depth classes~(G/L/C/M). \emph{Complementarity}: under perfectness and chain-level nondegeneracy (satisfied for all standard families; conditional in general), factorisation homology decomposes as $H^*(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g, Z(A)) \simeq Q_g(A) \oplus Q_g(A^!)$, a Lagrangian splitting.

Volume~II lifts this from one to three dimensions. The bar differential~$D_\cA$ is holomorphic factorisation on~$\C$; the deconcatenation coproduct~$\Delta$ is topological factorisation on~$\R$; together they form an $E_1$ dg coassociative coalgebra on $\FM_k(\C) \times \Conf_k^<(\R)$. The $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ structure emerges in the chiral derived center: the pair $(\cC^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA),\,\cA)$ is the bulk-boundary datum. The modular characteristic~$\kappa(\cA)$ gives the genus-one curvature; its chain-level continuation to $g \ge 2$ is conditional on the modular-bootstrap $H^2$ acyclicity and curved-Dunn comparison hypotheses. Complementarity lifts to the bulk-boundary-line triangle, and the discriminant~$\Delta$ classifies the complexity of bulk-boundary pairs. Classical Koszul duality is genus~$0$ on the formal disk; Volume~I is genus~$g$ on a curve; Volume~II is the dimensional lift to $\C \times \R$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The open primitive.}\enspace
The primitive object is the open/closed factorization dg-category~$\cC$
on the bordified curve $\widetilde{X}_D$
(\S\ref{subsec:categorical-open-sector}); the bar complex is its
coalgebraic shadow. This category's objects are boundary conditions;
its morphisms are open-string states. A choice of vacuum boundary
condition~$b$ produces a boundary algebra $A_b = \End_\cC(b)$ as a
\emph{chart}; the bar complex $\barB(A_b)$ encodes how closed-sector
operations twist the open-sector composition. The Morita class
of~$\cC$ is the invariant. Three objects are independently constructed
from~$\cC$ and must never be conflated:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the \emph{bar complex} $\barB(A_b)$ classifies twisting morphisms (couplings between~$A_b$ and its line-side/open-colour Koszul dual~$A^!_{\mathrm{line}}$);
\item the \emph{chiral derived center} $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cC) = \RHom_{\operatorname{Fun}(\cC,\cC)}(\operatorname{Id}, \operatorname{Id})$ is the universal bulk;
\item the \emph{line-side/open-colour Koszul dual algebra} $A^!_{\mathrm{line}} = D_{\operatorname{Ran}}(\barB(A_b))$, which later models the line side on the chirally Koszul locus.
\end{itemize}
Modularity is trace plus clutching on the open sector: the cyclic
trace on $\End(b)$ seeds a Calabi--Yau structure; the annulus
identification $\int_{S^1} \cC \simeq \HH_\bullet(\cC)$ is the first
modular shadow; and clutching (composing along shared boundary circles
via nodal degeneration) raises genus. The genus expansion of the
modular Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta^{\mathrm{oc}}_\cA$ is the
algebraic modular tower. The shadow archetypes~G/L/C/M classify the
$A_\infty$ complexity of the boundary algebra; in the gravitational
examples below, each archetype determines a distinct perturbative
structure.

\section*{II.\quad The Heisenberg on $\C \times \R$}

Take $\cA = \cH_k$: one generator $J$, one OPE
$J(z)\,J(w) \sim k/(z-w)^2$. Two operators sit at $(z_1, t_1)$
and $(z_2, t_2)$ with $t_1 < t_2$. Their bar element is
$[s^{-1}J \,|\, s^{-1}J]$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The holomorphic direction.}\enspace
The bar differential is the codimension-$1$ boundary operator
on $\FM_k(\C)$, the Fulton--MacPherson compactification (the iterated real blowup of $\C^k$ along all diagonal strata). For two insertions, the boundary stratum
$E_{12} \cong S^1$ of the FM blowup extracts the mode products
$J_{(n)}J$ from the OPE. The only nonvanishing mode is
$J_{(1)}J = k$ (the level), so
\[
d_{\barB}[s^{-1}J \,|\, s^{-1}J]
\;=\;
J_{(1)}J
\;=\; k.
\]
There is no simple-pole OPE ($J_{(0)}J = 0$), so the bracket
component of the bar differential vanishes. The entire
holomorphic interaction is the scalar~$k$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The topological direction.}\enspace
Cut $\R$ at each possible gap:
\begin{align*}
\Delta[s^{-1}J \,|\, s^{-1}J]
&\;=\;
[\,] \otimes [s^{-1}J | s^{-1}J]
&&\text{(cut before $t_1$)}
\\
&\;+\;
[s^{-1}J] \otimes [s^{-1}J]
&&\text{(cut between $t_1$ and $t_2$)}
\\
&\;+\;
[s^{-1}J | s^{-1}J] \otimes [\,]
&&\text{(cut after $t_2$)}.
\end{align*}
The middle term places one field on each half-line. Since
$J_{(0)}J = 0$, the two separated fields do not interact: the
factorisation is exact.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The coderivation property.}\enspace
Apply $D_\cA$ to the left-hand side:
$D_\cA[s^{-1}J | s^{-1}J] = k$, then
$\Delta(k) = k \otimes [\,] + [\,] \otimes k$.
Apply $(D_\cA \otimes \id + \id \otimes D_\cA)$ to the
right-hand side: only the first and third summands contribute
(the middle term $[s^{-1}J] \otimes [s^{-1}J]$ gives zero because
$D_\cA(s^{-1}J) = 0$), producing $[\,] \otimes k + k \otimes [\,]$.
Both sides agree. The collision in $\C$ and the cut in $\R$
commute.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The configuration-space product.}\enspace
The holomorphic collision lives on $\FM_2(\C)$. The topological
cut lives on $\Conf_2^<(\R) = \{t_1 < t_2\} \cong \R_{>0}$,
which is contractible. The bar element
$[s^{-1}J | s^{-1}J]$ is parametrised by the product
\[
\FM_2(\C) \times \Conf_2^<(\R).
\]
The differential sees the first factor. The coproduct sees the
second. The coderivation property is the independence of the two
factors. The same decomposition holds at degree~$n$:
\[
\bar{B}^{\mathrm{ch},n}(\cA)
\;\subset\;
(s^{-1}\bar\cA)^{\otimes n}
\otimes
\Omega^\bullet\bigl(\FM_n(\C) \times \Conf_n^<(\R)\bigr).
\]
The ordered configuration space $\Conf_n^<(\R) \cong
\R^{n-1}_{> 0}$ is contractible; its $E_1$-coalgebra structure
contracts to the strict coassociative coproduct: deconcatenation.
The topological coproduct alone introduces no higher homotopies. The
coproduct is the unique
coassociative coproduct on $T^c(V)$, determined by the
cogenerators.


\medskip\noindent\textbf{The Feynman transform and 't~Hooft expansion.}\enspace
Volume~I organises every genus-tower structure through the
convolution principle: the modular convolution dg Lie algebra
$\gAmod := \Hom_\Sigma(F\!\Com,\, \End_{\barB(\cA)})$
carries a dg Lie bracket from operadic composition, and
its universal MC element
$\Theta_\cA := D_\cA - d_0$ encodes the full genus tower.
For the Heisenberg algebra~$\cH_k$, the MC element collapses to a
scalar: $\Theta_{\cH_k} = k \cdot \eta \otimes \Lambda$, and its
genus-$g$ projection is
$F_g(\cH_k) = k \cdot \lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$.
Here $F\!\Com$ is the Feynman transform of the commutative
modular operad $\Com$ (with $\Com(g,n) = \Bbbk$ for all stable $(g,n)$), generated by stable graphs with edge-contraction
differential ($D^2 = 0$ from $\partial^2 = 0$ on
$\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$).

The associative variant
${\gAmod}^{E_1} := \Hom_\Sigma(F\!\Ass,\,
\End_{\barB^{E_1}(\cA)})$
($F\!\Ass$ is the Feynman transform of the associative modular operad, with ribbon graphs as summands)
replaces stable graphs by \emph{ribbon graphs} (fatgraphs)
and is the algebraic form of the 't~Hooft $1/N$ expansion:
$\hbar_{\mathrm{genus}} = 1/N^2$, the MC element is
$\sum_g N^{2-2g}F_g(\lambda)$, planar diagrams dominate at
large~$N$, and the coinvariant projection
$\Theta^{E_1} \mapsto \Theta$ is the passage from
colour-ordered to full amplitudes.

Volume~II promotes this to a two-coloured convolution algebra: $\SCchtop$ couples the $\C$-direction (Feynman transform, holomorphic) to the $\R$-direction (coproduct, topological), and the MC element $\alpha_T \in \MC(\gSC_T)$ encodes both.

\section*{III.\quad The Swiss-cheese operad $\SCchtop$}

The product $\FM_k(\C) \times \Conf_k^<(\R)$ is the local model for
the operations of the directional two-coloured dioperad $\SCchtop$:
\begin{alignat*}{2}
&\text{closed-to-closed:}\quad
&&\SCchtop(\mathsf{ch}^k;\,\mathsf{ch})
\;=\; \FM_k(\C), \\
&\text{mixed-to-open:}\quad
&&\SCchtop(\mathsf{ch}^k, \mathsf{top}^m;\,\mathsf{top})
\;=\; \FM_k(\C) \times E_1(m), \\
&\text{open-to-closed:}\quad
&&\SCchtop(\ldots, \mathsf{top}, \ldots;\,\mathsf{ch})
\;=\; \varnothing.
\end{alignat*}
Closed colour: holomorphic collisions on $\FM_k(\C)$. Open colour:
topological orderings on $E_1(m) = \Conf_m^<(\R)$. Mixed: the
product. Composition is FM substitution on the closed factor,
interval insertion on the open factor. The empty open-to-closed
component is directionality: bulk restricts to boundary, not
conversely.

The bar complex $(\barBch(\cA), D_\cA, \Delta)$ is an $E_1$ chiral
coassociative coalgebra: the differential encodes holomorphic
factorization, the coproduct encodes topological factorization.
The $\SCchtop$ structure emerges in the chiral derived center:
the pair $(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA),\, \cA)$ carries the
two-coloured Swiss-cheese datum.
Dioperadic Koszul duality for $\SCchtop$ exchanges the closed colour
($\mathsf{Com} \leftrightarrow \mathsf{Lie}$) while preserving the
open colour ($\mathsf{Ass}$)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:SC-self-duality}); the duality functor on
$\SCchtop$-algebras is an involution.  The open-colour dual
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ inherits an $(\SCchtop)^!$-algebra
structure. The verification
for the Heisenberg algebra is the four-step proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rosetta-e1-coalgebra}, carried out in the Rosetta
Stone chapter.

The closed colour is the Koszul direction (holomorphic collisions on~$\C$); the open colour is the groupoid direction (topological cuts along~$\R$). Directionality (the empty open-to-closed component) reflects the Springer pattern: representations restrict from $G$ to $B$, not conversely.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Homotopy-Koszulity.}\enspace
The classical Swiss-cheese operad $\mathsf{SC}$ ($E_2$ closed, $E_1$ open) is Koszul (Livernet).
Kontsevich formality enters through an associator-dependent zigzag
from the local $\mathsf{SC}$ model to the chiral-topological
dioperadic model. Bar-cobar transfer gives the homotopy equivalence
\[
\Omega\mathbf{B}(\SCchtop) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \SCchtop.
\]
The theorem is a homotopy-Koszulity statement in the coloured
dioperadic/properadic ambient, with the stated product-decomposition
and associator hypotheses (Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}).


\section*{IV.\quad The $\Ainf$ chiral operations}

The bar differential $D_\cA$ is a coderivation of the cofree
coalgebra $T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)$. A coderivation on a cofree
coalgebra is determined by its cogenerator projections: a sequence
of multilinear maps
\[
m_k \colon A^{\otimes k} \longrightarrow
A((\lambda_1))\cdots((\lambda_{k-1})),
\qquad k \ge 1, \quad \deg m_k = 2 - k,
\]
where $\lambda_j = z_j - z_k$ are spectral parameters recording
holomorphic separations. The identity $D_\cA^2 = 0$ expands into
the $\Ainf$ Stasheff relations: for each~$n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preface2-stasheff}
\sum_{\substack{i+j = n+1 \\ i,j \ge 1}}\;\sum_{s=0}^{n-j}
\pm\; m_i\bigl(a_1, \ldots, m_j(a_{s+1}, \ldots, a_{s+j}),
\ldots, a_n\bigr) \;=\; 0,
\end{equation}
with spectral substitution $\lambda_s \mapsto \Lambda_I$ when a
consecutive block $I$ collapses.

Each relation is Stokes' theorem on $\FM_n(\C)$.

\subsection*{The $n = 1$ relation: $m_1^2 = 0$}

$\FM_1(\C)$ is a single point (one marked point, no moduli).
There is no boundary: $\partial\FM_1(\C) = \varnothing$.
The Stasheff relation at $n = 1$ is
\[
m_1(m_1(a)) \;=\; 0,
\]
which says that $m_1 = Q$ is a differential: $Q^2 = 0$. This is
the BRST condition.

\subsection*{The $n = 2$ relation: the Leibniz rule}

$\FM_2(\C)$ modulo translations is $\C^\times$; after positive real
scaling its reduced boundary direction is $S^1$.  The real ordered
Stasheff locus for two inputs is a point. The boundary
$\partial\FM_2(\C)$ is a single collision stratum $z_1 = z_2$.
The Stasheff relation at $n = 2$ is
\[
m_1(m_2(a,b)) + m_2(m_1(a),b) +
(-1)^{|a|} m_2(a,m_1(b)) \;=\; 0,
\]
which says that $m_1 = Q$ is a graded derivation of~$m_2$:
the binary product is a chain map.

\subsection*{The $n = 3$ relation: the homotopy-associativity
identity}

The Stasheff associahedron $K_3$ is a closed interval $[0,1]$,
the quotient of $\FM_3(\C)$ by the affine group.
Its two endpoints are the two maximal collision strata:
$D_{12}=\{z_1\to z_2\}$ at one end,
$D_{23}=\{z_2\to z_3\}$ at the other.
The $n=3$ Stasheff relation is:
\[
\underbrace{m_2(m_2(a,b),c) - m_2(a,m_2(b,c))}_{%
 \text{associativity defect}}
\;=\;
\underbrace{m_1(m_3(a,b,c)) + m_3(m_1(a),b,c)
\pm\cdots}_{n=3:\;m_3\text{~interpolates}}\,.
\]
The binary product $m_2$ is associative up to the homotopy~$m_3$.
The operation $m_3$ is the integral over $K_3$:
it interpolates between the two boundary contributions
$D_{12}$ and $D_{23}$. When $m_3=0$ (Heisenberg, free
fermion), the relation reduces to strict associativity.
When the transferred operation $m_3$ is nonzero, the homotopy is the
Jacobiator on $K_3$.  For affine Kac--Moody the transferred minimal
operations vanish for $k \ge 3$ in the one-loop Koszul model; the
non-central simple-pole SC class is a separate chiral operation, not a
defect in the Lie Jacobi identity.  For Virasoro and class~M examples,
nonzero higher transferred operations record non-formality.

\subsection*{Higher $n$: the full Stasheff hierarchy}

At $n=4$, the associahedron $K_4$ is a compact polygon
(the Stasheff pentagon, five boundary faces from the
collision strata $D_{12}, D_{23}, D_{34}, D_{123}, D_{234}$).
The Stasheff relation involves $m_2$, $m_3$, and $m_4$,
	with $m_4$ arising as the integral over the interior of~$K_4$. For the
	$\mathcal{W}_3$ algebra, $m_4$ is nonzero and encodes the quartic
	contact interaction through the normalized $WW\Lambda$ projection
	\(P_\Lambda\{W_\lambda W\}=(32/(22+5c))\lambda\Lambda\).

The general pattern: $\FM_n(\C)/\mathrm{Aff}(\C)$, the Fulton--MacPherson
space modulo translations, dilations, and rotations (the full complex
affine group), is a compact manifold with corners of real
dimension $2n - 4$.%
\footnote{The quotient by the full complex affine group
$\mathrm{Aff}(\C) = \C \rtimes \C^*$ gives real dimension
$2(n{-}1) - 2 = 2n{-}4$. See Remark~\ref{rem:FM-convention}
for the three FM conventions used in this volume
(full: $\dim_\R = 2(n{-}1)$; reduced: $\dim_\R = 2n{-}3$;
affine: $\dim_\R = 2n{-}4$).
The Stasheff associahedron $K_n$ is a real $(n{-}2)$-dimensional
submanifold-with-corners inside $\FM_n(\C)/\mathrm{Aff}(\C)$,
obtained by restricting to the real locus $\FM_n(\R)$.}
Its full boundary strata are indexed by complex collision patterns.
The Stasheff relation uses the real ordered locus $K_n$, whose faces
are consecutive nested planar blocks.  All-subset strata belong to the
full $E_2$/FM relations; their restriction to the planar real locus
gives the $A_\infty$ Stasheff faces.  The identity
$\partial^2 = 0$ on the relevant compactified chain is the
combinatorial identity that makes $D_\cA^2 = 0$ hold.


\section*{IV$'$.\quad The resolvent principle}

For a strict binary chiral input equipped with a fixed deformation
retract, the transferred $\Ainf$ operations are determined by the
binary product~$m_2$ relative to that contraction.  A general
$A_\infty$ algebra may have independent higher input vertices; the
formula below is the homological perturbation formula in the binary
case.

Let $(H, p, \iota, h)$ be a deformation retract of $(\cA, m_1)$
onto cohomology $H = H^\bullet(\cA, m_1)$, with $h^2 = h\iota =
ph = 0$. Define the \emph{dressed propagator}
$\Phi \colon T^+(H) \to \cA$ by
\[
\Phi \;=\; \iota \;+\; h \circ m_2 \circ \Phi^{\otimes 2}.
\]
This is the \emph{tree-level bootstrap equation}: the full
propagator from boundary data~$H$ into the bulk algebra~$\cA$ is
determined self-consistently from the free embedding~$\iota$ and
the interaction vertex~$m_2$. Its solution by iteration gives the
\emph{tree formula}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:resolvent-principle}):
\[
m_k^H
\;=\;
\sum_{T \in \mathrm{PRT}_k}
p \circ \mathfrak{m}(T) \circ \iota^{\otimes k},
\]
where $\mathrm{PRT}_k$ is the set of planar rooted binary trees
with $k$~leaves ($|\mathrm{PRT}_k| = C_{k-1}$, the Catalan
number), and $\mathfrak{m}(T)$ assigns $m_2$ to each internal
vertex and~$h$ to each internal edge.

One equation, one binary input, Catalan many trees, all relative to
the chosen contraction. The Stasheff relations are the consistency of
the iteration; the tree formula is the perturbative expansion of the
Lagrangian self-intersection. Under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-wheel-bridge}, wheel diagrams in the Virasoro
case are evaluations of this tree sum using the BRST homotopy of
Drinfeld--Sokolov reduction.


\section*{V.\quad First computation: the Heisenberg}

\noindent\textbf{Operations.}\enspace
$m_1 = 0$ (no internal differential).
$m_2(J,J;\lambda) = k\lambda$ (the double-pole residue).
$m_k = 0$ for $k \ge 3$ (no composites, no higher poles).
The algebra is formal: the $\Ainf$ structure is concentrated in
$m_2$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{PVA on cohomology.}\enspace
On $H^\bullet(\cH_k) = \C[J]$, the binary operation decomposes
into a commutative product and a $\lambda$-bracket:
\[
\{J_\lambda J\} = k\lambda.
\]
This is a Poisson vertex algebra (PVA) of rank~$1$: a commutative differential algebra equipped with a $\lambda$-bracket $\{a_\lambda b\}$ satisfying sesquilinearity, skew-symmetry, the Jacobi identity, and the Leibniz rule.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Classical $r$-matrix.}\enspace
The bar complex uses the propagator $d\log(z-w)$, which absorbs one pole order from the OPE: the OPE $J(z)J(w)\sim k/(z-w)^2$ has a double pole, and the $d\log$ kernel reduces this to a simple-pole collision residue. The classical $r$-matrix is therefore
\[
r(z) \;=\; \frac{k}{z}.
\]
(The formal Laplace transform of the full $\lambda$-bracket $\{J_\lambda J\}=k\lambda$ gives $\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda z}k\lambda\,d\lambda = k/z^2$, which is the position-space OPE kernel; the collision residue is the simple-pole coefficient $k/z$ after the $d\log$ absorption, in the same way the Kac--Moody section below separates $\Omega/z$ from the $kK_\fg/z^2$ term.)
The quantum $R$-matrix: $R(z) = \exp(k\hbar/z)$. Yang--Baxter
holds trivially (the exponential of a scalar commutes with itself).

\section*{IX.\quad Koszulness}

The prototype for Koszul duality as convolution is the Chriss--Ginzburg theorem: the Borel--Moore homology of the Steinberg variety $\widetilde{\mathcal{N}} \times_{\fg} \widetilde{\mathcal{N}}$ (where $\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}$ is the Springer resolution of the nilpotent cone) recovers the Hecke algebra $H_W$ of the Weyl group:
$H_*^{BM}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}} \times_{\fg}

a convolution algebra. The bar complex $\barBch(\cA)$ on
$\FM_n(\C) \times \Conf_n^{<}(\R)$ is the coalgebraic convolution

$\FM_n(\C)$; the coproduct is restriction on $\Conf_n^{<}(\R)$;

line-sector operations, the $R$-matrix, the genus tower, and the
PVA\@; the later chirally-Koszul comparison theorems recover the
open-colour Koszul dual and line-category package.

the convolution yields the corresponding quantum-group line package:
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}}^{\mathrm{red}}|_{\mathrm{eval}} \simeq
\mathrm{Rep}_q(\fg)$ and the colored Jones
polynomial is recovered from the monodromy of the twisting morphism classified by the bar complex (Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-monodromy-identification},
Corollary~\ref{cor:jones-polynomial}).

\emph{Koszulness is the condition that this presentation is
concentrated.}

When the bar cohomology is concentrated ($H^{p,q}(\barBch(\cA)) = 0$
for $q \neq 0$), the derived self-intersection
$\Steinb_b := \barBch(\cA) \times_\cA^h \barBch(\cA)$
is \emph{clean}: the excess Tor sheaf
$\cT\!\mathit{or}^{\cO_\cA}_q(\cO_{\barBch},\cO_{\barBch})$
vanishes for $q \neq 0$, the Borel--Moore homology is
one-dimensional per stratum, and every invariant projects as
a scalar. Non-concentration means excess $\cT\!\mathit{or}_q
\neq 0$: the self-intersection carries higher derived structure,
$\Ainf$ operations proliferate, and curvature and braiding
entangle. In this sense, chiral Koszulness is a transversality
condition on the Lagrangian self-intersection.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The bar complex as a bigraded object.}\enspace
The bar complex carries two gradings: the \emph{internal degree}
(cohomological degree within~$\cA$) and the \emph{bar degree}
(number of tensor factors minus one). The bar differential mixes
these gradings: it lowers bar degree by one and raises internal degree
by the pole order of the OPE. The resulting spectral sequence, the
PBW spectral sequence of Volume~I, converges to the bar cohomology.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The concentration condition.}\enspace
The bar cohomology $H^{p,q}(\barBch(\cA))$ is bigraded. Say $\cA$
is \emph{chirally Koszul} if this cohomology is concentrated in a
single line:
\[
H^{p,q}\bigl(\barBch(\cA)\bigr) = 0 \quad \text{for } q \neq 0.
\]
This is a single condition. Twelve consequences follow from it,
each expressing a different face of the same concentration.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The twelve faces.}\enspace
The Koszul polytope of Volume~I:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Concentration}: bar cohomology in one degree.
\item \emph{Spectral collapse}: the PBW spectral sequence degenerates
 at~$E_2$. The associated graded $\gr_F \cA$ is classically Koszul
 (Priddy--BGS), and the chiral deformation introduces no corrections.
\item \emph{SC-formality, when present}: on the formal sublocus
$m_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$. One-loop exactness means multi-particle
collisions on $\FM_k(\C)$ contribute no new cohomology beyond what
the pairwise OPE already sees.  Class~M is chirally Koszul but not
SC-formal.
\item \emph{Small resolution}: the bar-cobar counit
 $\Omegach(\barBch(\cA)) \xrightarrow{\sim} \cA$ is a
 quasi-isomorphism. Verdier duality on the bar coalgebra gives
 $\mathbb{D}_{\operatorname{Ran}} \barBch(\cA) \simeq
 \barBch(\cA^!)$, identifying the Verdier dual with the bar of the
 Koszul dual algebra (Theorem~A intertwining).
 This chain-level identification is restricted to the Koszul,
 conilpotent, completed locus.  Outside that locus one has coderived
 inversion, and $\barBch(\cA^!)$ carries genuinely higher operations.
\item \emph{Ext diagonal}: $\Ext^{p,q}_\cA(\omega_X, \omega_X) = 0$
 for $p \neq q$ (the BGS criterion chiralized).
\item \emph{Hochschild concentration}:
 $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^n(\cA) = 0$ for $n \notin \{0, 1, 2\}$.
\item \emph{Factorization homology}: $\int_{\Sigma_g}\cA$ is
 concentrated in degree~$0$ in the theorem's genus range.
\item \emph{Barr--Beck--Lurie}: the comparison functor for the
 bar-cobar monad is an equivalence on the fiber; abstract monadicity
 applies without correction.
\item \emph{Kac--Shapovalov regularity}: $\det G_h \neq 0$ in the
 bar-relevant range; no singular vectors intrude to create unexpected
 cohomology classes.
\item \emph{Stratum acyclicity}: restriction to every FM boundary
 stratum is acyclic outside degree~$0$.
\item \emph{Lagrangian transversality}: the deformation and
 obstruction complexes $\mathbf{Q}_g(\cA)$ and $\mathbf{Q}_g(\cA^!)$
 are complementary Lagrangians
 (Vol~I, Theorem~C, the Lagrangian splitting of genus-$g$ cohomology;
 conditional on the shifted-symplectic hypothesis package).
\item \emph{Hodge purity}: $\barB_n(\cA)$ is pure of weight~$n$
 as a mixed Hodge module on $\FM_n(X)$ (forward implication proved; converse open).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Classical specialization.}\enspace
When $X = \mathrm{pt}$, the chiral tensor product is the ordinary
tensor product, the FM compactification is the Stasheff
associahedron, and all twelve criteria collapse to the classical
Koszul theory of Priddy--BGG--BGS--Loday--Vallette. The chiral theory
is a \emph{lift} that reveals their geometric origin. Concentration of
bar cohomology, in the classical setting, is a combinatorial miracle.
In the chiral setting, it is a consequence of Arnold cancellation on
FM compactifications: the boundary strata produce exact sequences,
and Koszulness is the statement that these exact sequences have no
higher cohomology. The ``miracle'' of classical Koszulness is the
shadow of the geometry of configuration spaces.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The examples.}\enspace
The Heisenberg is Koszul: $m_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$
(Section~V), the PBW spectral sequence has nothing to degenerate, and
$\cH_k^! = \mathrm{Sym}^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*)$ is the chiral symmetric algebra on the dual.

Kac--Moody $\widehat{\fg}_k$ is Koszul: the PBW spectral sequence
degenerates at~$E_2$ because $\gr_F \widehat{\fg}_k$ is a polynomial
algebra (classically Koszul by Priddy). The chiral Koszul dual is
$\mathrm{CE}^{\mathrm{ch}}(\widehat{\fg}_{-k-2h^\vee})$, or
equivalently the same-family scalar partner at the dual level after
passing to the scoped comparison surface. The big and small
resolutions agree on the Koszul locus.

Virasoro is chirally Koszul (the PBW spectral sequence concentrates),
but its Swiss-cheese structure is \emph{non-formal}: the transferred
operations $m_k^{\mathrm{SC}} \neq 0$ for all $k \geq 3$
(Section~VII). The big resolution $(\mathrm{Vir}_c)^!_\infty$
carries the infinite tower of wheel diagrams; the small Koszul dual
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$ is its shadow. The boundary between formal
and non-formal Swiss-cheese structure is controlled by the pole
structure of the OPE: simple poles give formal algebras;
higher-order poles create the higher operations that break formality.

Shadow depth and Koszulness are independent invariants.
Four depth classes emerge, each with a distinct
self-intersection geometry
(\S\ref{sec:shadow-tower-atlas}): class~$\mathbf{G}$ (clean
intersection, depth~$2$: Heisenberg, lattice VOAs),
class~$\mathbf{L}$ (Lie-transverse, depth~$3$: affine
$V_k(\fg)$, where Jacobi kills the quartic),
class~$\mathbf{C}$ (contact, depth~$4$: $\beta\gamma$), and
class~$\mathbf{M}$ (infinite excess tower, depth~$\infty$:
Virasoro, $\cW_N$). Depth classifies the \emph{complexity}
of the shadow obstruction tower, not the existence of a small resolution.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Homotopy-Koszulity of the operad.}\enspace
Individual algebras may have formal or non-formal
Swiss-cheese structure; the \emph{dioperad} $\SCchtop$
is homotopy-Koszul in the coloured dioperadic/properadic ambient.
Homotopy-Koszulity (Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}) is a property
of the categorical framework, not of any particular algebra. It is
what makes the bar-cobar framework of the 3d MC element~$\alpha_T$ of
Section~VIII available for targets satisfying the theorem's
completion hypotheses: $\mathbf{B}(\SCchtop)$ has $\partial^2 = 0$
after the stated transfer, while the six projections of
$\alpha_T$ are the closed/open/mixed/cohomological/genus components
coming from the colour and degree decomposition of the Swiss-cheese
convolution algebra
\textup{(}Definition~\textup{\ref{def:sc-convolution}},
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:3d-universal-mc}}\textup{)}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Koszulness and PVA descent.}\enspace
On the SC-formal part of the Koszul locus, the cohomological shadow
of the bar complex is exact: the higher $m_k$ vanish on cohomology,
and $m_2$ alone survives. The PVA structure of the next section is
therefore the complete invariant only on this formal sublocus. For
algebras with non-formal Swiss-cheese structure
(Virasoro, $\mathcal{W}_3$), the PVA is only the leading term of an
infinite tower: the higher transferred $m_k$ carry information beyond
the PVA, and quantization must lift the entire tower.


\section*{X.\quad PVA descent}
\label{sec:preface-pva-descent}

The $\Ainf$ operations $m_k$ are chain-level data. On the formal
affine-Heisenberg PVA descent locus, the transferred higher
operations vanish on cohomology and $m_2$ alone survives.  For class-M
objects such as Virasoro and principal $\mathcal W_N$, the PVA is the
leading cohomological shadow; a higher transferred tower remains
invisible to the PVA and must be tracked separately.

\subsection*{Decomposition of $m_2$}

The surviving operation $m_2(a,b;\zeta)$ is a formal Laurent
series in the collision variable $\zeta = z_1 - z_2$. Decompose
it into regular and singular parts:
\[
m_2(a,b;\zeta)
\;=\;
\underbrace{m_2^{\mathrm{reg}}(a,b;\zeta)}_{\text{no pole at }
\zeta = 0}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\sum_{n \ge 0}
\frac{a_{(n)}b}{\zeta^{n+1}}}_{\text{singular: the OPE}}.
\]
The regular part at $\zeta = 0$ is a commutative product:
\[
\mu(a,b) \;:=\; m_2^{\mathrm{reg}}(a,b;0).
\]
The singular part, after Borel transform
$\zeta^{-n-1} \mapsto \lambda^{(n)}$, is a $\lambda$-bracket:
\[
\{a_\lambda b\}
\;:=\;
\sum_{n \ge 0} \lambda^{(n)}\,a_{(n)}b.
\]
Together, $(\mu, \{-_\lambda-\})$ form a $(-1)$-shifted Poisson
vertex algebra on $H^\bullet(A, Q)$.

\subsection*{The Jacobi identity from $\FM_3(\C)$}

The Jacobi identity for $\{-_\lambda-\}$ is the $n = 3$ Stasheff
relation~\eqref{eq:preface2-stasheff} projected to cohomology.
Write it out. The boundary $\partial\FM_3(\C)$ has three
codimension-$1$ strata:
\[
D_{12} \;=\; \{z_1 \to z_2\}, \qquad
D_{23} \;=\; \{z_2 \to z_3\}, \qquad
D_{13} \;=\; \{z_1 \to z_3\}.
\]
Each stratum contributes one term to the $n = 3$ relation:
\begin{align*}
D_{12}&\colon\quad
m_2\bigl(m_2(a,b;\zeta_{12}),\, c;\, \zeta_{13}\bigr),
\\
D_{23}&\colon\quad
m_2\bigl(a,\, m_2(b,c;\zeta_{23});\, \zeta_{12}\bigr),
\\
D_{13}&\colon\quad
-\,m_2\bigl(m_2(a,c;\zeta_{13}),\, b;\, \zeta_{12}\bigr).
\end{align*}
The sign on $D_{13}$ is the Koszul sign from permuting $b$ past
$c$. Stokes' theorem on $\FM_3(\C)$ gives
$\sum_S \Res_{D_S}(\omega) = 0$ for any closed form~$\omega$ on
$\FM_3(\C)$. Applying this to the form built from three OPE
kernels, projecting to the singular parts, and performing the Borel
transform gives the $\lambda$-bracket Jacobi identity:
\[
\{a_\lambda \{b_\mu c\}\}
\;-\;
\{b_\mu \{a_\lambda c\}\}
\;=\;
\{\{a_\lambda b\}_{\lambda+\mu} c\}.
\]
The three boundary faces of $\FM_3(\C)$ are three collisions; the
cancellation of the integral over $\partial\FM_3(\C)$ is the Jacobi
identity.

\subsection*{Leibniz, skew-symmetry, and the exchange cylinder}

Leibniz ($\{a_\lambda bc\} = \{a_\lambda b\}c +
b\{a_\lambda c\}$) follows from the factorisation of the regular
part~$\mu$ through the collision stratum: when $z_2 \to z_3$ while
$z_1$ is far away, $m_2(a, m_2^{\mathrm{reg}}(b,c;0); \zeta_{12})$
decomposes into the product of $m_2(a,b;\zeta_{12})$ with~$c$ plus
$b$ with $m_2(a,c;\zeta_{13})$.

Skew-symmetry ($\{a_\lambda b\} = -\{b_{-\lambda-\partial} a\}$)
follows from the exchange automorphism $z_1 \leftrightarrow z_2$ on
$\FM_2(\C)$, which reverses the sign of $\zeta = z_1 - z_2$ and
introduces $\partial$ from the Jacobian of the coordinate change.

\subsection*{Heisenberg and Kac--Moody PVAs}

For the Heisenberg PVA the underlying commutative differential
polynomial algebra is $\C[J,\partial J,\partial^2J,\ldots]$.  The
singular bracket is
$\{J_\lambda J\} = k\lambda$. Jacobi is
vacuous (single generator).

For Kac--Moody the underlying product is the commutative
differential-polynomial product on
\(\operatorname{Sym}(\C[\partial]\otimes\fg)\). The
$\lambda$-bracket $\{J^a_\lambda J^b\} = f^{ab}{}_c J^c +
k\delta^{ab}\lambda$ recovers the affine PVA\@. Jacobi is the
Lie-algebra Jacobi identity for $f^{ab}{}_c$, which is the
residue of the three-face Stokes identity on $\FM_3(\C)$ evaluated
on the $\widehat{\fg}_k$ OPE.


\section*{XI.\quad The bulk-boundary-line triangle}

A holomorphic--topological theory on $\C \times \R_{\ge 0}$
produces three algebraic objects: \emph{bulk} local operators
$\Abulk$ (interior), \emph{boundary} local operators $\Bbound$
(on $\{0\} \times \C$), and \emph{line} operators
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}}$ (along $\R_+ \times \{0\}$).
On the chirally Koszul locus (boundary-linear exact sector),
the strongest relation is
\[
\Abulk
\;\simeq\;
\Zder(\Bbound),
\qquad
\cC_{\mathrm{line}} \simeq \cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-mod}.
\]
Unconditionally, bulk identifies with chiral Hochschild cochains
of the boundary algebra; the derived-center and line-module
identifications retain the scoped hypotheses above.

The \emph{bulk} vertex is the derived centre
$\Zder(\Bbound) :=
\RHom_{\Bbound\text{-}\Bbound}(\Bbound,\,\Bbound)$.
For rank-one Heisenberg:
$\Zder(\cH_k)=C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cH_k,\cH_k)$ with
cohomology $\C\oplus\C[-1]\oplus\C[-2]$ on the Vol~I concentration
surface.
For Kac--Moody at $k = -h^\vee$: the Feigin--Frenkel centre
$\mathfrak{z}(\widehat{\fg}_{-h^\vee})$, the algebra of
${}^L\!G$-opers. The \emph{line} vertex is modeled, on the
chirally Koszul locus, by $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules:
for Heisenberg,
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} = Y(\mathfrak{u}(1)) \simeq \cH_{-k}$
(Fock modules); for Kac--Moody,
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} = \Ydg(\fg)$.

\subsection*{The spectral $R$-matrix and Yang--Baxter}

Two parallel line operators $L_1(z_1)$, $L_2(z_2)$ interact
through the bulk, producing a meromorphic braiding
$R_{L_1, L_2}(z) \colon L_1 \otimes L_2
\xrightarrow{\sim}
L_2 \otimes L_1$ in $z = z_1 - z_2$.
Three lines braided in two orders give the Yang--Baxter equation
\[
R_{12}(z_{12})\,R_{13}(z_{13})\,R_{23}(z_{23})
\;=\;
R_{23}(z_{23})\,R_{13}(z_{13})\,R_{12}(z_{12}),
\]
from the same $\FM_3(\C)$ Stokes mechanism as the PVA Jacobi
identity: the Jacobi identity is the closed-colour projection,
the Yang--Baxter equation is the open-colour projection, and both
are shadows of the single $n = 3$ Stasheff identity.

For Heisenberg: $R(z) = e^{k\hbar/z}$ (scalar, YBE trivial).
For Kac--Moody on evaluation modules: the spectral $R(z)$ agrees
with the quantum group $R$-matrix of $U_\hbar(\fg)$, with
$r(z) = k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$ as classical limit.


\section*{XII.\quad PVA quantization: from classical to quantum}

The PVA $\{-_\lambda -\}$ on $H^\bullet(A, Q)$ is the
\emph{classical} shadow of the $\Ainf$ chiral algebra. The
quantization problem is the reverse: given a PVA, construct the
$\Ainf$ chiral algebra whose cohomological descent recovers it.

This is a lifting problem. Start with a PVA
$(V, \cdot, \{-_\lambda -\})$. The bracket
$\{a_\lambda b\} = \sum_{n \ge 0} \lambda^{(n)}\,a_{(n)}b$
encodes the singular part of the OPE; the product
$a \cdot b$ encodes the regular part. Together they determine
$m_2(a,b;\lambda)$ on cohomology. The quantization question: does
there exist an $\Ainf$ chiral algebra~$A$ with $H^\bullet(A) = V$
and $m_2|_{H^\bullet} = \mu + \{-_\lambda-\}$?

The obstruction to lifting lives in the Hochschild cohomology
$\HH^3_{\mathrm{ch}}(V)$: the failure of the Jacobi identity
to hold at the chain level, after the first-order lift, is a
cocycle whose class is the obstruction. When the obstruction
vanishes, the lift exists and is controlled by a Maurer--Cartan
element in the chiral deformation complex
$\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{ch}}(V)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Heisenberg quantization.}\enspace
The PVA $\{J_\lambda J\} = k\lambda$ lifts to the Heisenberg
$\cH_k$ with $m_2(J,J;\lambda) = k\lambda$ and $m_{k \ge 3} = 0$.
The obstruction vanishes: $\HH^3_{\mathrm{ch}}(\C[J]) = 0$
(the PVA is freely generated). The quantization is unique.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Kac--Moody quantization.}\enspace
The affine PVA $\{J^a_\lambda J^b\} = f^{ab}{}_c J^c +
k\delta^{ab}\lambda$ lifts to $\widehat{\fg}_k$. The
Lie Jacobi identity makes the first obstruction vanish.  In the
one-loop Koszul affine model the transferred higher operations vanish;
the non-central simple-pole SC class is a separate affine
factorisation datum.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Khan--Zeng 3d sigma model.}\enspace
Khan--Zeng construct a 3d holomorphic--topological sigma model for
finite-type PVA targets satisfying their analytic SDR hypotheses. The
boundary algebra of this theory is the corresponding quantization of
the PVA\@. The BV-BRST complex of the sigma model
on $\C \times \R_{\ge 0}$, with the PVA target data,
produces the $\Ainf$ operations $m_k$ by Feynman-diagram evaluation
on $\FM_k(\C)$: each $k$-ary operation is a sum of tree Feynman
diagrams with $k$ external legs and vertices weighted by the PVA
brackets. The Stasheff identity is Stokes' theorem on the boundary
of the Feynman-integral compactification, the same boundary
cancellation that gives the $\Ainf$ relations from $\FM_k(\C)$.

This sigma-model construction gives the physical construction of the
quantization theorem in the stated analytic class: the PVA data
determine a consistent field theory, and the field theory produces
the $\Ainf$ algebra. The mathematical formulation is the bar-cobar
correspondence of Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}; uniqueness is
the uniqueness inside the chosen homotopy class. Arbitrary
PVA targets require a separate existence theorem.


\section*{XIII.\quad The holographic construction}

A three-dimensional holomorphic--topological quantum field theory on
$\R_+ \times \C$ has bulk observables on the interior, boundary
observables on $\{0\} \times \C$, and line operators along
$\R_+ \times \{0\}$.

\subsection*{Twisted holography}

A 3d $\cN = 2$ gauge theory on $\R_+ \times \C$ with gauge
group~$G$ and Chern--Simons level~$k$ has an action
\[
S \;=\; \frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\R_+ \times \C}
\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(
 A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A
\bigr)
\;+\; S_{\mathrm{matter}}.
\]
The holomorphic--topological twist selects the supercharge
$Q = Q_+ + \bar{Q}_-$, under which the gauge field decomposes into
a holomorphic component $A_z \,dz$ and a topological component
$A_t \,dt$. The BV-BRST complex of the twisted theory on
$\R_+ \times \C$ has:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Boundary observables} ($t = 0$): the chiral algebra
 $\Bbound$ on~$\C$. For pure CS: $\Bbound = \widehat{\fg}_k$.
\item \emph{Bulk observables} ($t > 0$): the BRST cohomology
 $\Abulk = H^\bullet(C_{\mathrm{BV}}, Q)$.
 In the physical HT comparison, bulk observables identify with
 chiral Hochschild cochains of the boundary algebra; the stronger
 derived-center form $\Abulk\simeq\Zder(\Bbound)$ holds in the
 boundary-linear exact sector.
\item \emph{Line operators} ($z = 0$, extended along~$\R_+$):
 modules for the open-colour Koszul dual
 $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$. For pure CS at level~$k$, the
 chiral dual is $\cA^!_{\mathrm{ch}} =
 \mathrm{CE}^{\mathrm{ch}}(\widehat{\fg}_{-k-2h^\vee})$, while on the
 chirally Koszul affine locus the open-colour dual is the
 dg-shifted Yangian $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} = \Ydg(\fg)$,
 whose modules model the line category.
\end{itemize}
On evaluation modules in the affine lineage, the reduced HT
spectral $R$-matrix agrees with the quantum group $R$-matrix of
$U_q(\fg)$ at $q = e^{i\pi/(k+h^\vee)}$
\textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:affine-monodromy-identification}}\textup{)}.
Thus the line category is modeled by modules for the
open-colour Koszul dual, which on the chirally Koszul affine
locus carries the dg-shifted Yangian package.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Abelian Chern--Simons.}\enspace
$G = U(1)$: $\Bbound = \cH_k$,
$R(z) = e^{k\hbar/z}$, $\Abulk = \cH_k$ (bulk $\simeq$ boundary);
$\alpha_T = m_2 + \hbar k \eta \otimes \Lambda$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Non-abelian Chern--Simons.}\enspace
$G = SL_2$: $\Bbound = \widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_k$,
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{ch}} = \widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_{-k-4}$,
$r(z) = k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$. The KZ connection is the degree-$(2,0)$
projection of $\alpha_T$; on evaluation modules, the reduced HT
spectral $R$-matrix agrees with the quantum-group $R$-matrix of
$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at $q = e^{i\pi/(k+2)}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-monodromy-identification}).
Line category:
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}}^{\mathrm{red}}|_{\mathrm{eval}}
\simeq \operatorname{Rep}_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:affine-line-category});
colored Jones polynomial from bar-complex monodromy
(Corollary~\ref{cor:jones-polynomial}).

\subsection*{The holographic modular Koszul datum}

The 3d MC element $\alpha_T$ packages the holographic system:
closed sector $\to$ boundary algebra $\Bbound$ with genus tower
$\Theta_{\Bbound}$; open sector $\to$ line operations on
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}}$ (modeled by
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules on the chirally Koszul locus);

\;=\;

\subsection*{Anomaly completion}

At genus $1$, the bar differential is curved:
$d^2 = \kappa(\Bbound) \cdot \omega_1$ (Vol~I, Theorem~D).
For $g \ge 2$, the chain-level curved-Dunn continuation requires the
modular-bootstrap $H^2$ acyclicity and comparison hypotheses.
The \emph{transgression algebra} absorbs this curvature: adjoin
$\eta$ of degree~$1$ with
$\eta b = (-1)^{|b|} b \eta$ and
$d\eta = \Theta = \kappa \cdot \omega_1$, keeping $\eta^2 \ne 0$:
\[
B_\Theta
\;=\;
\frac{B * \Bbbk\langle\eta\rangle}
{\langle \eta b - (-1)^{|b|} b\eta \rangle},
\qquad
d\eta = \Theta.
\]
A $B_\Theta$-module is a $B$-module with a neutralisation
$h_M$ satisfying $dh_M = \Theta_M$: the datum extending
genus-$0$ structure through genus~$1$.

The \emph{secondary anomaly} $u := \eta^2$ is closed and central,
governing the genus-$g$ Clifford completion
$\mathfrak G_g(B_\Theta)$ (handle operators with
$\alpha_i \beta_j + \beta_j \alpha_i = \delta_{ij} \eta^2$,
encoding the intersection form on $H_1(\Sigma_g, \Z)$):
\begin{itemize}
\item Away from $u = 0$:
 $\mathfrak G_g[\eta^{-2}]
 \cong \operatorname{Mat}_{2^g}(B_\Theta[\eta^{-2}])$;
 higher genus is \emph{Morita-invisible}.
\item On $u = 0$:
 $\mathfrak G_g / (\eta^2) \cong B/(\Theta) \otimes
 \Lambda^{2g+1}$; exterior-algebra degeneration.
\end{itemize}
The full development is in
Chapter~\ref{app:anomaly-completed-topological-holography}.

embeds the half-space into its double
$X_{\mathrm{dbl}} = \C \times \R$ via the reflection
$\sigma(z,t) = (\bar z, -t)$:
\[
K^{\mathrm{refl}}(p_i, p_j)
\;=\;
K(p_i, p_j) \;+\; K(p_i, \sigma(p_j)).
\]
The reflected weight identity is the $\sigma$-invariant
restriction of the whole-space Stokes identity. The chain:
\[
\text{logarithmic $\SCchtop$-algebra}
\;\xRightarrow{\;\text{double}\;}
\text{reflected weight identity}
\;\xRightarrow{\;\text{operadic Stokes}\;}
\text{boundary vertex algebra}.
\]
Every finite-type logarithmic $\SCchtop$-algebra satisfying the
analytic SDR, finite-jet, inner-translation, and reflected-weight
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-half-space-bv} has a boundary
vertex algebra. The affine PV sigma model is logarithmic $\SCchtop$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:affine-is-log-SC}); DS reduction preserves
the structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:ds-preserves-SC}), so
Virasoro and $\mathcal{W}_N$ inherit the logarithmic SC shadow
(Corollary~\ref{cor:virasoro-wn-are-SC}). The standard landscape
is covered in this argument only through the affine bridge and DS
transport hypotheses.

\subsection*{The modular bar complex as genus expansion}

The modular bar differential
$D = \sum_{g \ge 0} \hbar^g D^{(g)}$, with $D^{(g)}$
a sum over stable graphs of genus~$g$ weighted by
$1/|\Aut(\Gamma)|$, is the Feynman transform of the modular
operad $\{\overline{\cM}_{g,n}\}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:formal-genus-expansion}). The canonical MC
element $\delta_\cA = D - D^{(0)}$ is the total positive-genus
correction (Theorem~\ref{thm:coderivation-MC-synthesis}). The
combinatorial structure (stable graphs, automorphism weights,
genus weights) is the same structure that appears in the 't~Hooft
expansion. The precise identification between the genus parameter
$\hbar_{\mathrm{genus}}$ and a power of $N$ is model-dependent; fixing
it for a specific holographic model is the principal open problem
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:thooft-gl-n}).

\subsection*{The relative holographic deformation complex}

The holographic lifting problem is controlled by the
\emph{relative fibre}
$\mathfrak{h}_T^\Theta :=
\operatorname{fib}(p\colon\gSC_T\to\gAmod)_{\alpha_T}$
over the fixed boundary datum $\Theta_\cA$. Its graded
obstruction tower
$[\mathfrak{o}_r(\beta)] \in
H^2(\operatorname{gr}^F_r\mathfrak{h}_T^\Theta)$
measures the order-by-order cost of extension; the
\emph{relative quartic obstruction}
$\mathfrak{R}_4^{\mathrm{rel}}(T)$ is the first invariant
detecting when a boundary datum cannot arise from a
planar holographic realisation.

\subsection*{Cyclic trace and the literal 't~Hooft
expansion}

A cyclic trace~$\tau$ on the open $E_1$-colour turns

\emph{ribbonised modular Swiss-cheese theory}
$\mathrm{RSC}_{\mathrm{tr}}^{\mathrm{mod}}$.
The \emph{Loop--Connes principle}
$\beta_{\mathrm{der}}^*(\Delta_{\mathrm{open}}) = \Lambda_P$:
closed genus creation is the image of the open BV operator
under the derived-centre map.

\subsection*{The arithmetic face (Vol~I)}

The shadow obstruction tower carries arithmetic content through the flat

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Holographic reconstruction.}\enspace
The chiral algebra fixes a formal local bulk model. The

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The completeness question.}\enspace
The holographic Lagrangian $\cL_\cA^{(0)}$ is constructed from genus-$0$ data: the bar-cobar adjunction on $\FM_k(\C) \times \Conf_k^<(\R)$. \emph{Is the holographic Lagrangian complete?} That is, does $\cL_\cA^{(0)}$ extend to a family $\cL_\cA^{(g)} \hookrightarrow \cM_{\mathrm{vac}}$ over the moduli $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$, compatible with gluing along boundary strata? For algebras with clean self-intersection (classes~$\mathbf{G}$ and~$\mathbf{L}$: Heisenberg, Kac--Moody), the Lagrangian meets itself transversally and the scalar curvature $d^2 = \kappa(\cA) \cdot \omega_g$ extends automatically in the rank-one cases (abelian Heisenberg, $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$); for multi-generator non-abelian rank~$\ge 2$ affine Kac--Moody at $g \ge 3$, the identification of $d^2$ with the scalar class $\kappa(\cA)\cdot \omega_g$ requires the scalar-diagonal hypothesis (Definition~\ref{V1-def:scalar-diagonal-hypothesis}) that the bar curvature decomposes along the scalar channel of the generator-rank filtration. For algebras with excess self-intersection (class~$\mathbf{M}$: Virasoro, $\mathcal{W}_N$), the derived self-intersection carries higher Tor, the Swiss-cheese operations $m_k$ are successive obstruction classes, and the question is open. (Classes~$\mathbf{G}$, $\mathbf{L}$, $\mathbf{C}$ are chirally Koszul: the PBW spectral sequence concentrates and bar cohomology is concentrated. Class~$\mathbf{M}$ has infinite shadow depth and non-formal Swiss-cheese structure: the fourth-order pole forces nonvanishing transferred operations $m_k^{\mathrm{SC}} \neq 0$ for all $k \geq 3$, though the algebra is chirally Koszul (PBW spectral sequence concentrates) and well-defined Koszul duals exist. The difficulty for class~$\mathbf{M}$ is the non-formality: beyond the global self-intersection geometry, the big Koszul dual carries higher $\Ainf$ operations.) This is the central problem of the modular theory: existence, compatibility, and computation of the higher-genus Lagrangian, developed in Part~\ref{part:holography}.

Everything proved in both volumes begins from finite-order projections

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Open problems.}\enspace
The open problem list has five parts. First, $\mathcal{W}(p)$ triplet tempering is
\emph{conjectural}: the Zhu-bounded-Massey argument fails because
Gurarie 1993 and Flohr 1996 construct logarithmic-CFT amplitudes with
unbounded Massey products despite finite-dimensional Zhu algebra;
the tempered class is the non-logarithmic stratum (Virasoro,
$\mathcal{W}_N$, Schellekens, Monster, irrational cosets) and
$\mathcal{W}(p)$ is open pending the Adamovi\'c--Milas
character-amplitude bound. Second, the non-tempered stratum is
empty on the non-logarithmic C$_2$-cofinite standard landscape;
logarithmic $\mathcal{W}(p)$ remains outside this scope. Third, the
CY-C pentagon stratification is by generator-lattice rank
$\rho^{R_i} \in \{3, 12, 24\}$. The compact Hodge supertrace gives
the route-independent value~$0$; it is not the
Heisenberg--Mukai chiral scalar. Fourth, the
Tier-3 Bernoulli-witness set is
$\{37, 691, 811\}$; the primes
$\{1423, 3067, 23, 43, 419\}$ occur instead as
Riccati-arithmetic characteristic primes in $S_r$ numerators.
Fifth, super-Yangian complementarity has a proved supertrace lane:
for $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$ with $m\ne n$,
$h^\vee=m-n$ and
\[
  \kappa^{\str}(k)
  =
  \frac{(k+m-n)((m-n)^2-1)}{2(m-n)},\qquad
  k^!=-k-2(m-n),
\]
so $\kappa^{\str}(k)+\kappa^{\str}(k^!)=0$.  The proposed
Berezinian $\max(m,n)$ formula is only a conjectural central-series
twist, not a theorem, and it should not be identified with evaluation
of $\sBer(T(u))$ at $u=0$.  At $\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)$, Beisert 2007
gives \emph{three} central elements in the AdS/CFT extension; this is
separate from the supertrace cancellation. The remaining genuine
open problems are: chain-level $E_3$-topological
structure for class-$M$ principal algebras in an explicitly
topologized analytic model stricter than the full weight completion
(the raw direct-sum model is excluded; the weight-completed case is proved,
Theorem~\ref{thm:chiral-higher-deligne} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:bv-bar-class-m-weight-completed}); class-$L$
original-complex chain-level strictification via explicit
antighost-contact terms $\eta_1^{(\mathrm{i})}$,
$\eta_1^{(\mathrm{ii})}$ making
$[Q,\widetilde{G}_1] = T_{\mathrm{Sug}}$ strict at chain level; the
finite Riccati envelope and closed form of $\beta_N$ for $N \ge 4$
(Stirling gives tempering only after a finite envelope is supplied);
and the canonical pairing underlying super-complementarity.
