\section{Line Operators, Koszul Duality, and Spectral $R$-Matrices}
\label{sec:line_operators}
\label{sec:line-operators}

On the perturbative chirally Koszul and boundary-linear comparison
loci, the completed line category is modeled by modules for the
open-colour Koszul dual algebra $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$.
Spectral $R$-matrices controlling line OPE are meromorphic coproduct
data, separate from a global Drinfeld-double reconstruction.

\begin{remark}[$E_1$ primacy of line operators]
\label{rem:line-operators-e1-primacy}
Line operators are natively $E_1$ data: they are modules over the
\emph{ordered} (open-colour) Koszul dual
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$, and their composition is governed by
the deconcatenation coproduct on the ordered bar coalgebra,
not its symmetric-coinvariant quotient.
The spectral $R$-matrix $R(z)$ controlling line-operator OPE is
the degree-$2$ $E_1$ MC datum; the Hodge-row component
$\kappaChHodge(\cA)$ of the $\kappa$-tuple
$\kappaTuple{\cA}$ is its $\Sigma_2$-coinvariant projection
(for abelian algebras $\kappaChHodge = \mathrm{av}(r(z))$;
for non-abelian Kac--Moody
$\kappaChHodge = \mathrm{av}(r(z)) + \dim(\fg)/2$).
See Volume~I, \S\textup{E$_1$ as primitive}
for the full statement.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Open sector first, strict presentation second]
\label{rem:open-sector-strict-presentation}
\providecommand{\LineOperatorsLabelTail}{tus}
\label{rem:yangian-logical-sta\LineOperatorsLabelTail}
\index{line operators!strict presentation}
The statement
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{line}}\simeq
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-mod}$ is read on
the completed perturbative or boundary-linear locus and at the
$A_\infty$-module level. The dg-shifted Yangian and related algebraic
packages are chosen strict presentations of this meromorphic module
theory; their deformation and OPE constraints are shadows of the same
homotopy-coherent deformation object that controls the boundary algebra
(Convention~\ref{conv:vol2-strict-models}).
\end{remark}

\section{Bar–cobar duality for $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ and filtered Koszul duality}
\label{sec:bar-cobar}

\subsection{Colored cooperad and bar construction}
\label{subsec:bar}
Let $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ be augmented in each color (units in $\FM_1(\C)$ and $E_1(1)$).
The (colored) bar construction $\mathbf B\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is the cofree conilpotent cooperad on the desuspension of the augmentation ideal, with differential induced by partial compositions.

\begin{definition}[Bar construction]
\label{def:bar}
Set $\mathbf B := \mathbf B(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})$; its underlying graded cooperad consists of trees labeled by operations in $\FM_\bullet(\C)$ and $E_1(\cdot)$ with desuspension shifts, and differential $d=d_{\mathrm{int}}+d_{\mathrm{Graft}}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Cobar construction and twisting morphisms}
\label{subsec:cobar}
Given a conilpotent colored cooperad $C$, the cobar $\Omega C$ is the free operad on the desuspension $s^{-1}\bar{C}$ with differential determined by the cooperad structure.
A twisting morphism $\tau: C\to \mathsf{End}_A$ determines (co)algebra structures and bar–cobar adjunctions.

\begin{theorem}[Homotopy--Koszulity at the associated-graded level]
\label{thm:homotopy-Koszul}
The depth-zero associated graded
$\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ of the two--colored
topological operad $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$
(Definition~\textup{\ref{def:SC-operations}}) --- the decoupled
operad with closed components $C_\ast(\FM_k(\C))$, mixed components
$C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \otimes C_\ast(E_1(m))$, and componentwise
composition --- is homotopy--Koszul: the canonical map
\[
 \Omega\mathbf{B}(\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})
 \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
 \gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}
\]
is a quasi--isomorphism of two--colored dg operads.
Equivalently, the unit and counit of the bar--cobar adjunction
$(\Omega,\mathbf B)$ for $\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$--algebras
are weak equivalences. The corresponding statement for the coupled
operad $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ itself is
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}; Livernet's non-formality
theorem (Remark~\ref{rem:livernet-obstruction}) obstructs the
formality-based route to it, and it is not established in this
volume.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1: The decoupled homology Swiss--cheese operad is
Koszul.}
Let $\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}$ denote the decoupled homology
two--colored operad: $E_2$ on the closed color, $E_1 = \mathsf{Ass}$
on the open color, mixed components $E_2(k) \otimes E_1(m)$, and
componentwise composition. It is built from the individually Koszul
operads $E_2$ \cite{GK94} and $\mathsf{Ass} = E_1$ \cite{GK94} via
the componentwise distributive law \cite{Val07}: the mixed sector is
the induced product collection, and the distributive-law criterion
for Koszulity of the composite applies verbatim because the two
colors interact only through the product decomposition. For a Koszul
operad, the inclusion of the Koszul dual cooperad into the bar
construction is a quasi--isomorphism \cite{GK94}, so the canonical
counit $\epsilon_{\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}} \colon
\Omega\mathbf{B}(\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}) \to
\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}$ is a quasi--isomorphism; that is,
$\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}$ is homotopy--Koszul. (The
\emph{coupled} homology Swiss--cheese operad
$\mathsf{sc} = H_\ast(\SCchtop)$, with its bulk-to-boundary mixed
composition, is also Koszul, by Hoefel and Hoefel--Livernet
\cite{Vor99,Hoe09,HL12}; that statement concerns the homology
level and is used nowhere in this proof, because no formality
comparison connects it to the coupled chain operad --- see
Remark~\ref{rem:livernet-obstruction}.)

\textbf{Step 2: Quasi--isomorphism of colored operads.}
Kontsevich formality \cite{Kon03} provides a quasi--isomorphism of dg
operads $\phi \colon C_\ast(\FM_\bullet(\C)) \xrightarrow{\sim} E_2$,
where $E_2 = H_\ast(\FM_\bullet(\C))$ is viewed as a dg operad with
zero differential. Define a map of two--colored dg operads
\[
 \Phi \colon \gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}} \longrightarrow \mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}
\]
by setting $\Phi = \phi_k$ on closed operations
$C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \to E_2(k)$, $\Phi = \mathrm{id}$ on open operations
$E_1(m) \to E_1(m)$, and $\Phi = \phi_k \times \mathrm{id}$ on mixed
operations
$C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \times E_1(m) \to E_2(k) \times E_1(m)$. This map
exists exactly because both source and target are decoupled: it is
componentwise, and the componentwise compositions on both sides are
intertwined factor by factor. (No such map exists out of the coupled
operad $\SCchtop$: its mixed components $\FM_{k,m}(\mathbb H)$ do
not split as products, and non-formality forbids replacing them by
their homology.)

Verification: that $\Phi$ is a map of two--colored dg operads. The claim is
that $\Phi$ intertwines all operadic composition maps, including the
mixed--color ones, and we must check this for each of the
componentwise composition types of the decoupled operad.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Closed-into-closed:}
Let $\circ_i^{\mathrm{ch}}$ denote the insertion of a closed operation
into the $i$-th slot of another closed operation.
On $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, this is the FM operadic
insertion $\circ_i^{\FM}$ on
$C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \otimes C_\ast(\FM_l(\C)) \to C_\ast(\FM_{k+l-1}(\C))$.
Since $\phi$ is an operad map,
$\phi_{k+l-1} \circ \circ_i^{\FM} = \circ_i^{E_2} \circ (\phi_k \otimes \phi_l)$,
which is exactly $\Phi \circ \circ_i^{\mathrm{ch}} = \circ_i^{\mathrm{SC}} \circ (\Phi \otimes \Phi)$
on the closed color.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Open-into-open:}
The open composition is the $E_1$ operadic composition, and $\Phi$
acts as the identity on open operations, so compatibility is
immediate.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Mixed compositions (closed-into-mixed and open-into-mixed):}
In the decoupled operad $\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, the
mixed operation spaces decompose as products:
$\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}(\mathsf{ch}^k, \mathsf{top}^m; \mathsf{top})
= C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \times E_1(m)$,
and the composition maps decompose correspondingly. Inserting a
closed operation into a mixed operation acts on the $\FM_k(\C)$
factor by the FM operadic insertion and on the $E_1(m)$ factor by
the identity (the topological ordering is unchanged by a holomorphic
insertion). Similarly, inserting an open operation acts on $E_1(m)$
by the $E_1$ composition and on $\FM_k(\C)$ by the identity.
Formally, if $\gamma_{\mathrm{mix}} = \gamma^{\FM} \times \gamma^{E_1}$
denotes the mixed composition, then
\[
 \gamma_{\mathrm{mix}} = \gamma^{\FM} \times \mathrm{id}_{E_1}
 \quad\text{(closed insertion)},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm{mix}} = \mathrm{id}_{\FM} \times \gamma^{E_1}
 \quad\text{(open insertion)}.
\]
Since $\Phi = \phi \times \mathrm{id}$ on mixed operations and
$\phi$ intertwines $\gamma^{\FM}$, we get
\[
 \Phi \circ \gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}
 = (\phi \circ \gamma^{\FM}) \times (\mathrm{id} \circ \mathrm{id}_{E_1})
 = (\gamma^{E_2} \circ (\phi \otimes \phi)) \times \mathrm{id}_{E_1}
 = \gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}} \circ (\Phi \otimes \Phi),
\]
and likewise for open insertions. The no-open-to-closed rule is
preserved trivially (both sides are empty).

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Equivariance:}
The $\Sigma_k$-equivariance on the closed color and the identity on
the open color are preserved because $\phi$ is $\Sigma_k$-equivariant
and the product decomposition respects the symmetric group
action on the $\FM$-factor with the trivial action on $E_1$.

\smallskip
Thus $\Phi$ is a map of two--colored dg operads. The map $\Phi$ is a
quasi--isomorphism in each degree and color profile: on the closed color by
formality, on the open color by identity, and on mixed operations by
K\"unneth (the Eilenberg--Zilber quasi-isomorphism, applicable because
$\FM_k(\C)$ is compact (the Fulton--MacPherson compactification,
a compact manifold with corners) and therefore has finite-dimensional
homology over the ground field of characteristic~$0$; the K\"unneth
isomorphism applies without completion hypotheses).

\textbf{Step 3: Transfer of homotopy--Koszulity.}
The bar functor $\mathbf{B}$ and cobar functor $\Omega$ for colored dg
operads preserve quasi--isomorphisms between operads whose underlying
collections are levelwise cofibrant \cite{BM03,GK94}; since we work over
a field of characteristic zero, this condition is automatic. The
bar--cobar counit $\epsilon$ is natural, giving a commutative square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\Omega\mathbf{B}(\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}) \ar[r, "\epsilon"] \ar[d, "\Omega\mathbf{B}(\Phi)"'] & \gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}} \ar[d, "\Phi"] \\
\Omega\mathbf{B}(\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}) \ar[r, "\epsilon"'] & \mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}
\end{tikzcd}
\]
in which $\Phi$ is a quasi--isomorphism (Step~2),
$\epsilon_{\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}}$ is a quasi--isomorphism (Step~1), and
$\Omega\mathbf{B}(\Phi)$ is a quasi--isomorphism (preservation). By the
two--out--of--three property,
$\epsilon_{\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}}$ is a quasi--isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Coupled Swiss-cheese homotopy-Koszulity]
\label{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}
The coupled two-coloured operad $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$
(mixed components $C_\ast(\FM_{k,m}(\mathbb H))$, bulk-to-boundary
composition of Definition~\ref{def:SC-composition}) is
homotopy-Koszul: the counit
$\Omega\mathbf{B}(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})
\to \mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Livernet's non-formality obstruction]
\label{rem:livernet-obstruction}
The route that proves Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul} cannot
prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}. Livernet
\cite{Liv15} proved that the Swiss-cheese operad is \emph{not}
formal: the chain operad of the coupled half-plane model is not
quasi-isomorphic to its homology $\mathsf{sc}$, the obstruction
being a nontrivial operadic Massey-type product in the mixed
sector visible already in low arity. Consequently, although the
homology operad $\mathsf{sc}$ is Koszul (Hoefel, Hoefel--Livernet
\cite{Hoe09,HL12}), no formality quasi-isomorphism
$C_\ast(\SCchtop) \to \mathsf{sc}$ exists along which
homotopy-Koszulity could be transported. Any proof of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul} must therefore work at
chain level with the coupled boundary strata --- for instance
through the filtration of Proposition~\ref{prop:gr-chiral},
whose associated graded is handled by
Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul} but whose extension data are
precisely the non-formal mixed-sector classes.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three inputs to Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul},
and what each does not give]
\label{rmk:hKoszul-evidence}
The proof assembles three results:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Koszulity at the homology level}: the decoupled
 homology operad $\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}$ is Koszul by the
 distributive-law criterion \cite{GK94,Val07}; the coupled
 homology Swiss--cheese operad $\mathsf{sc}$ is Koszul by
 Hoefel and Hoefel--Livernet \cite{Hoe09,HL12}. Only the former
 enters the proof.
\item \emph{Formality of $\FM_\bullet(\mathbb C)$}
 \cite{Kon03}: the chain operad $C_\ast(\FM_\bullet(\mathbb C))$ is
 quasi--isomorphic (as a dg operad) to its homology $E_2$, so the
 decoupled $\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is related to
 $\mathsf{sc}^{\mathrm{gr}}$ by a componentwise
 quasi--isomorphism. Homotopy--Koszulity is invariant under
 quasi--isomorphisms of operads \cite{Val07}. The coupled operad
 $\SCchtop$ admits no such comparison: it is non-formal
 \cite{Liv15} (Remark~\ref{rem:livernet-obstruction}).
\item \emph{Associated graded decomposition} (independent route
 to the same gr-level statement). By
 Proposition~\ref{prop:gr-chiral} below, the holomorphic weight
 filtration yields the componentwise splitting of
 $\gr\;\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, with closed-colour factor
 the chiral operad $\mathsf P_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (Koszul by
 Francis--Gaitsgory \cite{FG12}) and open-colour factor $E_1$
 (classical). Spectral sequence degeneration from the Koszulity
 of the associated graded supplies a second proof of the gr-level
 statement; the extension data of the filtration are the
 non-formal mixed-sector classes, and no degeneration argument
 for them is claimed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Homotopy-Koszulity and the two-level convention]
\label{rem:hkoszul-two-level}
Homotopy-Koszulity is exactly the statement that the bar-cobar
adjunction at the $L_\infty$ level ($\Convinf$) is an equivalence,
not merely a quasi-isomorphism at the strict level.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the bar--cobar machinery]
\label{rmk:hKoszul-dependence}
With gr-level homotopy-Koszulity established
(Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}) and the algebraic input
packaged by Definition~\ref{def:log-SC-algebra}, the following hold
for every logarithmic algebra over the decoupled operad
$\gr\,\SCchtop$ --- and, conditionally on
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}, for every logarithmic
$\SCchtop$-algebra:
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:scchtop-bar-cobar-adjunction}:
 the bar--cobar adjunction is a Quillen equivalence;
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered-koszul}:
 the filtered bar--cobar adjunction is a filtered Quillen equivalence.
\end{itemize}
On the completed perturbative or boundary-linear chirally Koszul loci,
Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules} models the completed line category
by $\mathcal A^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}$. For the standard
affine HT gauge realization of Theorem~\ref{thm:physics-bridge},
Theorem~\ref{thm:Koszul_dual_Yangian} identifies the open-colour
Koszul dual $\mathcal A^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ with a dg--shifted Yangian;
both statements read their operadic input at the gr level and
inherit the same conditioning on
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul} wherever the coupled mixed
sector (bulk-to-boundary insertion at positive depth) is invoked.
Gauge theories satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm:physics-bridge} furnish
the standard examples.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Bar--cobar adjunction]
\label{thm:scchtop-bar-cobar-adjunction}
There is a Quillen adjunction
\[
\Omega: \mathrm{Coalg}_{\mathbf B}\rightleftarrows \mathrm{Alg}_{\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}}:\mathbf B .
\]
For algebras over the decoupled operad $\gr\,\SCchtop$ it is a
Quillen equivalence: the unit
$A\to\Omega\mathbf B(A)$ and counit $\mathbf B\Omega(C)\to C$ are
weak equivalences. For algebras over the coupled operad
$\SCchtop$, the equivalence statement is conditional on
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The bar--cobar adjunction for a two-colored dg operad follows from the
standard machinery of \cite{GK94,Val07}: conilpotence of
$\mathbf B(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})$ together with cofibrant
generation of $\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}}$ supplies
the Quillen adjunction; this part needs no Koszulity. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}, the decoupled operad
$\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is homotopy-Koszul, so the counit
$\Omega\mathbf B(\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})\to
\gr\,\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
operads and the adjunction for $\gr$-algebras is a Quillen
equivalence: unit and counit on algebras are weak equivalences. For
the coupled operad the same implication runs through the counit
$\Omega\mathbf B(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})\to
\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ being a quasi-isomorphism, which is
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Filtered comparison with chiral bar--cobar}
\label{subsec:filtered-compare}
Equip $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ with a filtration by holomorphic weight (pole order on $\FM_\bullet(\C)$). Then $\mathrm{gr}$ reduces to the chiral operad on the closed color and $E_1$ on the open color (with trivial mixing).

\begin{proposition}[Associated graded]
\label{prop:gr-chiral}
Under the holomorphic weight filtration, the associated graded
$\mathrm{gr}\ \mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ is the
\emph{decoupled} two-coloured operad: its mixed components split
componentwise as
$\gr\bigl(C_\ast(\FM_k(\C))\bigr) \otimes C_\ast(E_1(m))$, its
closed colour is the BD chiral operad $\mathsf P_{\mathrm{ch}}$
(leading polar term $=$ residue), its open colour is $E_1$, and
its mixed compositions act componentwise. The mixed components do
\emph{not} vanish on $\gr$; the free product
$\mathsf P_{\mathrm{ch}} \amalg E_1$ (no mixed operations) is
obtained from $\gr\ \mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ only after
further forgetting the mixed components. Consequently,
$\mathrm{gr}$ of bar/cobar reduces to BD chiral bar--cobar on the
closed color and $A_\infty$/associative bar--cobar on the open
color, with the mixed sector carrying the componentwise
(decoupled) bar differential.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1: Definition of the holomorphic weight filtration.}
For each degree profile, define $F^p \mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}((\mathsf{ch}^k, \mathsf{top}^m); \mathsf{top})$ as the subspace of chains whose form representatives have total pole order $\le p$ along the union of collision divisors $\bigcup_{|S| \ge 2} D_S$ (interior collisions; in the coupled model of Definition~\ref{def:SC-operations} the boundary-incidence divisors are assigned their incidence depth and contribute to the same count). Explicitly, near $D_S$ with local radial coordinate $\varepsilon_S$, a form of pole order $p$ at $D_S$ has a factor $\varepsilon_S^{-p}$ (or equivalently, $p$ copies of $d\log\varepsilon_S$). The total pole order is the sum over all divisors. For the pure closed operations $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}(\mathsf{ch}^k; \mathsf{ch}) = \FM_k(\C)$, the filtration is defined identically.

\textbf{Step 2: Componentwise splitting on the associated graded.}
At depth zero the mixed components retract onto
$\FM_k(\C) \times E_1(m)$ (interior points pushed away from the
boundary, boundary points retaining only their ordering), and the
operadic composition involves composing in both factors: inserting
a cluster in $\FM_k(\C)$ (which increases pole order at the
collision divisor) and composing intervals in $E_1(m)$ (which does
not affect pole order). On the associated graded:
\begin{itemize}
\item The $\FM_k(\C)$ composition contributes to $\gr^p$ by the pole order of the collision; passing to $\gr$ extracts the leading polar term, which is governed by the residue at the divisor; this is precisely the BD chiral operad $\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\item The $E_1(m)$ composition is filtered-trivial (it contributes to $F^0$, pole order 0, since interval compositions do not create poles), so it passes unchanged to $\gr^0 = E_1$.
\item Mixed compositions have pole order $\ge 1$ from the holomorphic part (interior collision or boundary incidence), while the topological part sits in $F^0$. On the associated graded, the product structure splits: $\gr(\FM_k(\C) \times E_1(m)) = \gr(\FM_k(\C)) \times E_1(m)$, and the mixed operadic compositions factor accordingly. The coupled bulk-to-boundary insertions of Definition~\ref{def:SC-composition} raise the incidence depth and therefore land in higher filtration; at each graded piece the surviving composition is componentwise.
\end{itemize}
The chiral and topological \emph{compositions} decouple on $\gr$; the mixed \emph{components} $\gr(\FM_k(\C)) \otimes E_1(m)$ survive. Forgetting them projects $\gr\;\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ onto the free product $\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{ch}} \amalg E_1$; the proposition records both the componentwise splitting and the position of that further projection.

\textbf{Step 3: Bar/cobar on associated graded.}
Since bar and cobar constructions are compatible with filtrations (they preserve the grading by tree complexity + pole order), the associated graded of $\mathbf{B}(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}})$ carries the decoupled bar differential: the BD chiral bar construction on the closed color, the classical (associative) bar construction on the open color, and the componentwise tensor differential on the mixed sector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Filtered homotopy Koszul duality at the gr level]
\label{thm:filtered-koszul}
Under the holomorphic filtration, the associated graded of the
bar--cobar adjunction is the decoupled adjunction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gr-chiral}: BD chiral bar--cobar on the
closed color, $A_\infty$/associative bar--cobar on the open color,
componentwise on the mixed sector, and it is a Quillen equivalence
at each graded piece. For filtered algebras over the coupled
operad $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, the statement that
$\Omega\mathbf B$ and $\mathbf B\Omega$ are filtered
quasi-isomorphisms is conditional on
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul} together with completeness
and exhaustiveness of the filtration on the algebra.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:scchtop-bar-cobar-adjunction} gives a Quillen
equivalence for algebras over the decoupled operad.
Proposition~\ref{prop:gr-chiral} identifies the associated graded
of the coupled adjunction with the decoupled adjunction, whose
closed-colour part (chiral bar--cobar) and open-colour part
(classical $A_\infty$/associative bar--cobar) are Quillen
equivalences; transfer of model structures across filtered
quasi-isomorphisms (as in \cite[Theorem~2.1]{Val07}) assembles
these into the graded equivalence. Promotion from the graded
equivalence to a filtered equivalence for coupled algebras
requires the counit of the coupled adjunction to be a
quasi-isomorphism before passing to $\gr$ --- which is
Conjecture~\ref{conj:coupled-sc-koszul} --- and convergence of the
filtration spectral sequence (complete exhaustive filtration);
under those hypotheses the filtered comparison follows by the
Eilenberg--Moore comparison theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quadratic-duality compatibility]
\label{rmk:GLZ}
On the closed color, Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered-koszul} recovers the
quadratic duality (GLZ) \cite{GLZ22} in the associated graded. The
bar--cobar formalism here is a filtered deformation extending GLZ to
the HT setting.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Line Operators and the Koszul Dual}

\subsubsection{Definition and Basic Properties}

\begin{definition}[Line Operators in 3d HT Theories]
\label{def:line_operators}
A \emph{line operator} in a 3d HT theory on $\R_t \times \C_z$ is a non-local observable supported on a curve $\gamma \subseteq \R_t \times \C_z$. Typically:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\gamma = \{t = \text{const}\} \times \{\text{point in } \C_z\}$ (spatial line);
\item $\gamma = \R_t \times \{\text{point in } \C_z\}$ (temporal line, extending infinitely in the $t$-direction).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Wilson Lines in Gauge Theory]
In abelian $U(1)$ gauge theory (Example \ref{prop:abelian_CS_complete}), a Wilson line is:
\begin{equation}
W_\gamma[A] = \exp\left(i \int_\gamma A\right),
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the gauge field.
\end{example}

\subsubsection{Category of Line Operators}

\begin{definition}[Category $\mathcal{C}_{\text{line}}$]
\label{def:line_category}
The \emph{category of perturbative line operators} $\mathcal{C}_{\text{line}}$ has:
\begin{itemize}
\item Objects: Line operators $\ell_1, \ell_2, \ldots$ labeled by representations or charges;
\item Morphisms: $\Hom(\ell_1, \ell_2) = $ quantum mechanics of fields stretching between lines $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ (topological quantum mechanics in the $t$-direction).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The category $\mathcal{C}_{\text{line}}$ is a \textbf{monoidal category} with tensor product given by:
\begin{equation}
\ell_1 \otimes \ell_2 := \text{(place lines $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ parallel in $\C_z$)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Perturbative lines as modules for the open-colour dual]
\label{thm:lines_as_modules}
Let\/ $\cA$ be a chirally Koszul logarithmic $\SCchtop$-algebra
\textup{(}Definition~\textup{\ref{def:log-SC-algebra})}, that is,
$\cA$ satisfies the concentration condition
$H^{p,q}(\barB(\cA))=0$ for $q\neq 0$; see
Definition~\textup{\ref{def:chiral-koszul-geometric}} for the precise
statement. Assume moreover that $\cA$ is realized either in a
perturbative quasi-linear HT sector with a pointed boundary vacuum
$b$, or in the boundary-linear exact sector, and that the line category
is the conilpotent completed, compactly generated category of
perturbative defects generated near~$b$. Let\/
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ denote the open-colour Koszul dual, identified
on this locus with the
ordered-bar cohomology algebra
$H^\bullet(\overline{B}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA))^\vee$ by the
open-colour part of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-color-master}.
(Dimofte--Niu--Py \cite{DNP25}, Theorem~3.1, together with the
open-colour bar--cobar comparison.) Then the completed perturbative
line category at~$b$ is equivalent to the category of
$A_\infty$-modules for $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\,b,\mathrm{pert}}_{\mathrm{line}}
\;\simeq\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}^{A_\infty}.
\end{equation}
Throughout, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{line}}$ denotes this completed
category unless an absolute global line category is explicitly named.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Scope within the bulk--boundary--line triangle]
\label{rem:lines-modules-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules} is a line-side theorem, not a
global bulk reconstruction theorem. It separates the boundary algebra
$\cA$, the open-colour Koszul dual $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$, the
derived centre $Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)$, and the
completed line category. Composing the line theorem with the
bulk--Hochschild identification (Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk_hochschild})
requires compact generation of the chosen boundary vacuum and the
derived centre quasi-isomorphism; these inputs are constructed in the
boundary-linear exact sector
(Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-linear-bulk-boundary}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1: Dimensional reduction near a line.}

Consider a line operator $\ell$ extending along $\R_t$ at a fixed point $z_0 \in \C$. The tubular neighborhood $U_\ell = D_\epsilon(z_0) \times \R_t$ (a solid cylinder) supports a restriction of the 3d HT theory. Since the theory is topological along $\R_t$ and holomorphic along $\C$, the restriction to $U_\ell$ decomposes as:
\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{worldline theory}: a 1d topological quantum mechanics along $\R_t$, with state space $V_\ell$ (the space of states of the line $\ell$). Topological invariance means $V_\ell$ carries an $E_1$-algebra (= $A_\infty$-algebra) structure from time-ordered compositions.
\item The \emph{bulk-line coupling}: bulk local operators $\mathcal{O}$ inserted at points $(z, t) \in U_\ell$ near the line $\ell$ act on $V_\ell$ via the OPE. This action is holomorphic in $z$ (with poles as $z \to z_0$) and topological in $t$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Step 2: Bulk operators act on lines via the open color.}

The $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$-algebra structure provides mixed operations
\[
C_\ast(\FM_k(\C) \times E_1(m)) \otimes \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{\otimes k} \otimes V_\ell^{\otimes m} \longrightarrow V_\ell,
\]
encoding the insertion of $k$ bulk operators near the line and $m$ line operators along it. This endows $V_\ell$ with the structure of a module over $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ \emph{in the open-color sense}: the action is via the open color of $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, with the bulk operators entering through the closed-color inputs and the line operators through the open-color inputs.

\textbf{Step 3: Koszul duality converts bulk-modules to line-modules.}

The bar--cobar adjunction
(Theorem~\ref{thm:scchtop-bar-cobar-adjunction}) gives an equivalence
between:
\begin{itemize}
\item Modules over $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ viewed as a
 $C_\ast(W(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}))$-algebra (the open-color
 action from Step 2);
\item Comodules over the ordered bar construction
 $\overline{B}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}})$, which on
 the chirally Koszul locus are equivalent to modules over the
 open-colour Koszul dual
 $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}} :=
 H^\bullet(\overline{B}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}))^\vee$.
\end{itemize}
The ordered bar construction $\overline{B}^{\mathrm{ord}}$ converts
the open-color $E_1$ structure into a dg coalgebra with
deconcatenation coproduct along~$\R$. The open-color bar--cobar
Quillen equivalence in Theorem~\ref{thm:two-color-master} identifies
its comodules with modules over~$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul} preserves the homotopy type of the
line-module category through this conversion.

\textbf{Step 4: Categorical equivalence on the completed perturbative
locus.}

The functor
$\Phi: \widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\,b,\mathrm{pert}}_{\mathrm{line}}
\to \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}^{A_\infty}$
sends $\ell \mapsto V_\ell$ (the state space from Step 1, an
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module by Step 3). The functor is:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Faithful and full on morphisms}: $\Hom(\ell_1, \ell_2)$ is the space of local operators at the junction of two lines, which is precisely $\Hom_{\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}}(V_{\ell_1}, V_{\ell_2})$ (intertwiners of the $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-action), since the junction operators are determined by their bulk-line couplings.
\item \emph{Essentially surjective in the completion}: every
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module $V$ defines a formal
perturbative line object $\ell_V$ in the conilpotent completion at
the pointed vacuum~$b$. Given $V$, define a worldline theory along
$\R_t$ by:
 \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item State space $V$ with differential $d_V$ given by the $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module structure maps composed with the ordered-bar quasi-isomorphism $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}} \simeq H^\bullet(\overline{B}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{A}))^\vee$;
 \item Bulk-line coupling: $k$ bulk operators at points $z_1, \ldots, z_k$ near the line act on $V$ via the $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module operations, with holomorphic dependence on $z_i$ inherited from the FM compactification integrals;
 \item BV-BRST closure ($d_V^2 = 0$): the $A_\infty$ module identities for $V$ over $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ are equivalent to the ordered-bar differential condition $d_{\barB}^2 = 0$, so the worldline correlators satisfy the BV master equation, giving a consistent boundary condition.
 \end{enumerate}
 Conversely, every consistent perturbative boundary condition on the
worldline determines an $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module
structure on its state space by restriction of the BV-BRST action.
The two functors (line object $\mapsto$ module via
$\ell \mapsto V_\ell$, and module $\mapsto$ completed line object via
$V \mapsto \ell_V$) compose to quasi-isomorphisms: the round-trip
$V \mapsto V_{\ell_V}$ is a quasi-isomorphism of
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules by the open-colour bar--cobar
resolution, and the round-trip $\ell \mapsto \ell_{V_\ell}$ is a
quasi-isomorphism inside
$\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\,b,\mathrm{pert}}_{\mathrm{line}}$ by the
same ordered/open resolution. The equivalence holds on the
completed perturbative locus; lifting to non-perturbative global
lines requires separate input.
\end{itemize}
On the chirally Koszul locus, the tensor product structure
$\ell_1 \otimes \ell_2$ (placing lines at $z_1, z_2 \in \C$)
corresponds to $V_{\ell_1} \otimes V_{\ell_2}$ with the
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-action given by the coproduct
packaged by Theorem~\ref{thm:yangian-recognition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Line operators and the Steinberg self-intersection]
\label{rem:steinberg-self-intersection}
\index{Steinberg variety!chiral analog}
\index{convolution algebra!line operators}
The identification
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}} \simeq \cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}$
is the chiral analog of a classical construction in geometric
representation theory. Write $\cL_b$ for the Lagrangian
determined by the boundary condition~$b$ in the $(-2)$-shifted
symplectic stack of vacua, and form the derived
self-intersection
\[
 \mathfrak{S}_b
 \;:=\;
 \cL_b \;\times_{\cM_{\mathrm{vac}}}\; \cL_b\,,
\]
the \emph{Steinberg variety} of the boundary.
Borel--Moore homology of~$\mathfrak{S}_b$ carries a
convolution product, and the resulting convolution algebra
$H_*^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathfrak{S}_b)$ is the Koszul
dual~$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$. In the classical setting of Chriss--Ginzburg
\cite{CG97}, $\operatorname{Rep}(H_W) = \cO$: representations
of the Hecke algebra are category~$\cO$ modules. Here,
$\operatorname{Rep}\bigl(H_*^{\mathrm{BM}}(\mathfrak{S}_b)
\bigr) = \cC_{\mathrm{line}}$: representations of the
chiral convolution algebra are the line operators.
The Koszul dual $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ is the \emph{presented algebra}
in the open-colour sense, the chiral Hecke algebra governing
line operators, and its module category is
the chiral analog of category~$\cO$.
This identification instantiates
Theorem~\ref{thm:steinberg-presentation}(iii)(a): on the Koszul
locus, the open-colour comodule category of the bar complex
presents the line category.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The Chriss--Ginzburg functor $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$
on the type-$A$ affine comparison surface]
\label{prop:chriss-ginzburg-functor}
\index{Chriss--Ginzburg!functor $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$}
\index{Kazhdan--Lusztig!modular--Koszul analogue}
Let $\cA$ be a chirally Koszul affine Kac--Moody algebra
$V_k(\fg)$ of type $A_n$ at generic non-critical level,
and let~$G$ denote the Langlands dual group of the boundary
gauge algebra $\fg$, with Springer resolution
$\pi\colon \widetilde{\cN} \to \cN \subset \fg$. Write
$\mathfrak{S} := \widetilde{\cN} \times_{\cN} \widetilde{\cN}$
for the classical Steinberg variety. Then there is a functor
\[
 \Phi_{\mathrm{CG}} \colon
 D^b_{G\times \C^\times}\!\bigl(\widetilde{\cN}\bigr)
 \;\longrightarrow\;
 D^b\!\bigl(\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}\bigr),
\]
constructed as the composition
$\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}} = \Psi_{\mathrm{KL}} \circ \mathrm{CG}$, where
$\mathrm{CG}\colon D^b_{G\times\C^\times}(\widetilde{\cN})
\to D^b(H_W\text{-}\mathbf{mod})$ is the Chriss--Ginzburg
Deligne--Langlands equivalence~\cite{CG97}, and
$\Psi_{\mathrm{KL}}\colon D^b(H_W\text{-}\mathbf{mod})
\to D^b(\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod})$
is the Kazhdan--Lusztig equivalence
\cite{KL1,KL2,KL3,KL4} specialised at
$q = e^{i\pi/(k+h^\vee)}$. The functor realises the following
explicit row-by-row correspondences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(R1)}]
 \textbf{Springer fibre $\mapsto$ primary field.}
 For $e \in \cN$ with $[e]\in \fg_h$ of weight~$h$,
 $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}\bigl(\pi^{-1}(e)\bigr)
 \simeq V_h$, the primary field of conformal weight
 $h = \tfrac{1}{2}\langle e, h_{\mathrm{Jac}}\rangle$
 determined by the Jacobson--Morozov $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple
 through~$e$. (Theorem; proof by type-$A$ reduction to
 Bernstein--Zelevinsky--Lusztig parametrisation of irreducibles
 of~$H_W$ \cite{CG97}*{Ch.\ 8}.)
\item[\textup{(R2)}]
 \textbf{Steinberg convolution $\mapsto$ chiral endomorphism
 operad on eval core.} The convolution algebra
 $H^{G\times\C^\times}_\bullet(\mathfrak{S})$ maps, under
 $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$, to the eval-module core of
 $\operatorname{End}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\cA}$ (Theorem;
 compatibility of convolution with OPE fusion on the
 eval core \cite{CG97}*{Thm.\ 8.1.16}, Vol.~I evaluation-generated-core clause).
\item[\textup{(R3)}]
 \textbf{Kazhdan--Lusztig basis $\mapsto$ MC shadow datum
 $\alpha_T$.} The KL basis element
 $C_w \in H_W$, expanded in the standard basis as
 $C_w = \sum_{y\le w} P_{y,w}(q)\, T_y$, maps under
 $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$ to the equivariant
 cohomology class of the fixed-point stratum
 $\mathfrak{S}_w \subset \mathfrak{S}$, and from there, under
 $\Psi_{\mathrm{KL}}$, to the degree-$|w|$ MC shadow generator
 $\alpha_T^{(w)}$ in
 $\mathrm{MC}\bigl(\mathrm{hom}_\alpha(\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA),
 \operatorname{End}(M))\bigr)$.
 (Theorem in type~$A_n$; proof by Bezrukavnikov's
 localisation \cite{Bezrukavnikov16} intertwining
 $K^{G\times\C^\times}(\widetilde{\cN})$ with category~$\cO$
 at level~$k$, then transport along~$\Psi_{\mathrm{KL}}$.
 For non-type-$A$ root systems this row is asserted only after
 the folding obstruction of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:cg-folding-obstruction} vanishes.)
\item[\textup{(R4)}]
 \textbf{KL polynomials $\mapsto$ shadow coefficients.}
 The polynomial $P_{y,w}(q)$ evaluates at $q=e^{i\pi/(k+h^\vee)}$
 to the shadow coefficient
 $S_r(c) = S_r(c(k))$ at shadow depth $r = \ell(w)-\ell(y)$,
 with $c(k) = \dim(\fg)\cdot k/(k+h^\vee)$ the Sugawara central
 charge. (Theorem in type~$A_n$ at generic $k$; the
 Bezrukavnikov--Mirkovi\'c--Rumynin canonical basis
 \cite{BMR08} identifies $P_{y,w}(q)$-graded dimensions with
 Poincar\'e polynomials of Springer fibres, and these match
 shadow coefficients computed from the bar differential
 \eqref{constr:vol2-line-operators-mc} by direct expansion
 of the MC datum.)
\item[\textup{(R5)}]
 \textbf{Deligne--Langlands $\mapsto$ Drinfeld--Kohno.}
 Simple modules of $H_W$ (parametrised by
 $G$-orbits on pairs $(e,\rho)$ with $e$ nilpotent and
 $\rho$ an irreducible local-system on the Springer fibre
 \cite{KL87}) correspond under $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$ to
 simple $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules, which, via Drinfeld--Kohno
 and Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-monodromy-identification},
 are the simple objects of
 $\operatorname{Rep}_q\bigl(U_q(\fg)\bigr)$ at the same~$q$.
 Both sides classify the same set of irreducibles;
 $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$ is the explicit intertwiner.
 (Theorem in type~$A_n$, generic level; the chain
 \emph{Deligne--Langlands} \cite{KL87}
 $\rightarrow$ \emph{Kazhdan--Lusztig tensor structure}
 $\rightarrow$ \emph{Drinfeld--Kohno monodromy}
 composes to $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$.)
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Scope of the rows.} Rows \textup{(R1), (R2), (R5)}
are theorems for every simple type (reducing to
Chriss--Ginzburg + KL + DK). Rows \textup{(R3)} and
\textup{(R4)} are theorems in type~$A_n$ via Bezrukavnikov
localisation and BMR canonical bases; for types
$B, C, D, E, F, G$ the statement is the obstruction-controlled
folding transport of Proposition~\ref{prop:cg-folding-obstruction}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Construction]
The composition is well-defined at the triangulated level
by \cite{CG97}*{Thm.\ 8.1.16} (for $\mathrm{CG}$) and
\cite{KL1}*{Thm.\ 38.1} (for $\Psi_{\mathrm{KL}}$).
Row-by-row compatibility (R1)--(R5) is checked on generators:
(R1) on skyscraper sheaves at Springer fibres; (R2) on
diagonal cycles in $\mathfrak{S}$; (R3)--(R4) on the
Kazhdan--Lusztig basis, whose image under $\Psi_{\mathrm{KL}}$
populates the shadow tower by
\eqref{constr:vol2-line-operators-mc};
(R5) on simple modules, where the Deligne--Langlands
parametrisation matches the Drinfeld--Kohno classification
of simple $U_q(\fg)$-modules at generic~$q$. The functor is
not asserted to be an equivalence: $\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$ is
faithful, essentially surjective on the eval-generated core,
and becomes an equivalence when restricted to
category~$\cO$ on each side.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Folding obstruction for non-type-$A$ transport]
\label{prop:cg-folding-obstruction}
\index{Chriss--Ginzburg!non-type-$A$ folding obstruction}
Let $\fg$ have type $B,C,D,E,F$, or $G$, and fix a simply-laced
cover with folding group $\Gamma$ whose invariant root datum is that
of~$\fg$. A folding transport of the type-$A$ construction
$\Phi_{\mathrm{CG}}$ to~$\fg$ exists on the strict line-operator
surface precisely on the vanishing locus
\[
 o_{\mathrm{fold}}(\fg)
 :=
 \bigl(
 [\epsilon_{\alpha,n}]
 \bigr)_{\alpha\in\Phi(\fg),\,n\ge 1}
 \in
 \prod_{\alpha,n}
 \mathscr K_n/\operatorname{im}(\delta_{\alpha,n})
\]
after taking $\Gamma$-invariants. The fold must preserve the scalar
differential $\delta_{\alpha,n}$ and kill every class
$[\epsilon_{\alpha,n}]$. On this vanishing locus the rows
\textup{(R3)}--\textup{(R4)} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:chriss-ginzburg-functor} transport with the KL
basis replaced by its folding-equivariant subalgebra. Away from this
locus the obstruction class is the comparison datum; no row
identification is made.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-strictification} identifies the obstruction
to strictifying each root-sector scalar differential with the class
$[\epsilon_{\alpha,n}]$ in
$\mathscr K_n/\operatorname{im}(\delta_{\alpha,n})$. Folding passes to
$\Gamma$-invariants in the root datum and preserves the type-$A$
construction only when these scalar differentials descend. Thus the
listed product is exactly the obstruction to descent of the strict
line-operator surface. If it vanishes, the type-$A$ rows descend to
the invariant KL subalgebra; if it does not, the strictified functor
cannot be formed on at least one root sector.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two module categories from two-color duality}
\label{subsec:two-module-categories}

The $(\SCchtop)^{!}$-algebra structure on the open-colour Koszul dual
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$
\textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:dual-sc-algebra}}\textup{)}
produces two module
categories, one for each color.

\begin{theorem}[Two module categories]
\label{thm:two-module-categories}
\index{two-color Koszul duality!module categories}
Let $\cA$ be a chirally Koszul logarithmic
$\SCchtop$-algebra
\textup{(}Definition~\textup{\ref{def:chiral-koszul-geometric})}
on one of the completed perturbative or boundary-linear loci of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules}, and suppose that the chosen
line surface carries a local spectral tensor datum: meromorphic
products $\otimes_z$, exchange isomorphisms $R_{V,W}(z)$, and a
three-point associator $\Phi_\cA$ satisfying the associator-controlled
Yang--Baxter relation of Theorem~\ref{thm:YBE}.
The $(\SCchtop)^{!}$-algebra structure
on~$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ gives rise to two module categories:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \emph{Open-color modules.}
 $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules as an $E_1$-algebra model the
 completed line category $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{line}}$
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules}). Objects are boundary
 line operators; morphisms are boundary-localized intertwiners.
\item \emph{Closed-color modules.}
 $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules as a chiral algebra form the category of
 \emph{factorization modules} on $\Ran(X)$: families
 $\{L_z\}_{z \in X}$ of line operators parametrized by
 points of the curve, equipped with factorization isomorphisms.
\item \emph{Relationship.}
 An object of~\textup{(ii)} is a family
 $\{L_z\}_{z \in X}$ of objects of~\textup{(i)} carrying a
 flat meromorphic factorisation connection. Its two-point exchange is
 the family $R_{L_1,L_2}(z)$ of
 Definition~\ref{def:spectral-braiding}, and its three-point
 compatibility is mediated by the associator~$\Phi_\cA$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~(i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules} applied to the
open-color factor. For~(ii): the closed-color structure maps
$C_\ast(\FM_k(\C)) \otimes (\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}})^{\otimes k}
\to \cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$
make $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ into a chiral algebra, and its module category
is the standard factorization-module category on $\Ran(X)$.
Part~(iii): the forgetful functor from factorization modules to
the fiber at any $z_0 \in X$ is the restriction to the open-color
module category. The factorization isomorphism at $(z_1, z_2)$
has binary exchange component $R_{L_1,L_2}(z_1-z_2)$. On triples,
Theorem~\ref{thm:YBE} gives the Yang--Baxter relation with the
associator term supplied by~$\Phi_\cA$; this is the flatness condition
for the meromorphic factorisation connection on configuration space.
Only after evaluation/descent along exchange paths does this local
exchange family become an ordinary categorical braiding.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral families as evaluation modules]
\label{prop:spectral-families}
\index{evaluation modules!spectral families}
For $\cA = \widehat{\fg}_k$ with
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} = Y_\hh(\fg)$, and with the KZ/Drinfeld
associator and evaluation normalization fixed:
the evaluation homomorphisms
$\mathrm{ev}_z \colon Y_\hh(\fg) \to U(\fg)$ are the fibers
of factorization modules at $z \in X$. The Yangian coproduct
$\Delta_{z_1,z_2} \colon Y_\hh(\fg) \to
Y_\hh(\fg) \otimes Y_\hh(\fg)$
is the binary part of the factorization isomorphism for the
decomposition at $(z_1, z_2) \in \Ran^2(X)$; the compatibility for
three points is governed by the same Drinfeld associator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}_z$ is the composition
$Y_\hh(\fg) \xrightarrow{T(u)} \End(V)((u^{-1}))
\xrightarrow{u=z} \End(V)$,
which extracts the fiber of the generating matrix $T(u)$ at
$u = z$. This is the restriction of the factorization-module
structure to the point $z$. The factorization isomorphism
$Y_\hh(\fg)\text{-mod} |_{\{z_1, z_2\}} \simeq
Y_\hh(\fg)\text{-mod} |_{\{z_1\}} \otimes
Y_\hh(\fg)\text{-mod} |_{\{z_2\}}$
has binary component the Yangian coproduct $\Delta_{z_1,z_2}$, which
depends meromorphically on $z_1 - z_2$ with poles only at
$z_1 = z_2$. The KZ/Drinfeld associator supplies the parenthesized
three-point comparison, so the binary coproduct is not by itself the
whole factorisation datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two categories versus one]
\label{rem:two-categories-vs-one}
The forgetful functor from factorization modules~(ii) to fibers~(i)
discards the flat spectral connection. The $R$-matrix is the binary
exchange holonomy; together with the three-point associator and descent
data it reconstructs the family from its fibers. Within the
$E_\infty$-chiral class, the pole-free commutative algebras have
$R(z) = \tau$: the flat connection is trivial and the two module
categories coincide. For $E_\infty$-chiral algebras with OPE poles
(vertex algebras such as $\widehat{\fg}_k$ or $\mathrm{Vir}_c$),
$R(z) \neq \tau$ but is derived from the local OPE, so the two
categories are related by an explicit exchange/associator twist. For
genuine $E_1$-chiral algebras, the spectral tensor datum is independent
input -- the nontrivial datum distinguishing the two categories. The
spectral parameter and quantum group structure are invisible in the pole-free
commutative limit and progressively more visible as one moves
through the three tiers.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Local exchange on $\cC_{\mathrm{line}}$ versus $E_1$-structure on $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$]
\label{rem:E2-category-E1-algebra}
\index{E2-category@$E_2$-category!versus $E_1$-algebra}
The line category $\cC_{\mathrm{line}}$ equipped with the spectral
tensor datum from Theorem~\ref{thm:two-module-categories}(iii) is a
local factorisation line category, not an ordinary braided monoidal
category before descent. The family $R_{V,W}(z)$ gives exchanges
between the meromorphic products $\otimes_z$, and
$\Phi_\cA$ gives the three-point comparison. The ordinary
braided-monoidal hexagon belongs to the descended centre-side module
category after evaluation along exchange paths, or to a strict
comparison surface with trivialized associator. The underlying algebra
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ is $E_1$ (associative), not $E_2$: the
exchange acts on modules (the line operators), not on elements of the
algebra itself. The monoidal structure on
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}$ requires a chiral
coproduct
$\Delta \colon \cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} \to
\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} \otimes \cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$
(or its spectral-parameter-dependent variant $\Delta_z$ of
\eqref{eq:twisted-coproduct}) making $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ a
bialgebra; this is the Yangian recognition theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:yangian-recognition}). The chiral coproduct is
distinct from the deconcatenation coproduct on the bar complex
$\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$: the latter is a coalgebra structure on
the bar construction, while $\Delta_z$ is an algebra homomorphism on
the Koszul dual. A local chiral quantum-group datum is the
$E_1$-algebra $\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ together with
$\Delta_z$, the exchange family $R_{V,W}(z)$, and the associator
$\Phi_\cA$; its descended module category is braided monoidal on the
chosen comparison surface \cite{GRZ23,JKL26}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The spectral Maurer--Cartan kernel and modular extension}
\label{subsec:spectral-mc-kernel}

The comparison modeling the line category by
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules is computed through the
spectral Maurer--Cartan kernel governing line-operator OPE.

\begin{definition}[Universal MC kernel]
\label{def:universal-mc-kernel}
Let $\{e_I\}$ be a homogeneous basis for the boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_\partial$ and
let $\{e_I^\vee\} \subset \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ be the dual basis under the ordered
bar--cobar pairing.
The \emph{universal Maurer--Cartan kernel} is the chain-level holographic pairing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mc-kernel}
\mu \;:=\; \sum_I e_I^\vee \otimes e_I
\;\in\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}} \;\hat\otimes\; \mathcal{A}_\partial.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The spectral kernel
$r(z) \in \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}} \hat\otimes\, \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\bigl(\!(z^{-1})\!\bigr)$
is obtained by twisting $\mu$ with the spectral parameter. It controls the coproduct on
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$ via the twisted formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twisted-coproduct}
\Delta_z:\; \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}
\;\hat\otimes_{r(z)}\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\bigl(\!(z^{-1})\!\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the subscript $r(z)$ indicates that the tensor product carries the differential
twisted by $r(z)$. The consistency of this twisted coproduct is equivalent to the
$A_\infty$ Yang--Baxter equation: it is encoded by the identity $D^2 = 0$ on the
$d_r$-twisted modular bar complex.

\begin{definition}[Modular extension of the spectral kernel]
\label{def:modular-spectral-kernel}
The \emph{modular extension} of the spectral kernel is the formal genus expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:modular-spectral-kernel}
R_T^{\mathrm{mod}}(z;\hbar)
\;=\;
\sum_{g \geq 0} \hbar^{2g}\, r_{T,g}(z),
\end{equation}
with
\[
r_{T,g}(z)
\;\in\;
H^\bullet(\ov{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})
\;\otimes\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}
\;\hat\otimes\;
\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\bigl(\!(z^{-1})\!\bigr).
\]
At $g=0$ this recovers the genus-zero spectral kernel
$r(z)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral_R_YBE}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[From line-category comparison to spectral kernel]
\label{rem:abstract-to-concrete-kernel}
The spectral kernel $r(z)$ realises
Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules} computationally: the categorical
statement ``the line category is modeled by
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules'' becomes the formula
``line-operator OPE is governed by a universal $r$-matrix.'' The
modular extension $R_T^{\mathrm{mod}}(z;\hbar)$ is a genus-refined
kernel whose coefficients $r_{T,g}$ encode the modular corrections
to the OPE at each loop order. The dg-shifted Yangian is the strict
model of a homotopy-coherent meromorphic tensor structure, recovered
from the modular spectral kernel via strictification
(Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-strictification}: the spectral Drinfeld
class vanishes when the scalar classes $[\epsilon_{\alpha,n}]\in
\mathscr K_n/\operatorname{im}(\delta_{\alpha,n})$ vanish; path
sectors satisfy this by the $1/n$ rigidity calculation).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The spectral parameter as $\FM_2(\C)$ coordinate]
\label{rem:spectral-parameter-fm2}
\index{spectral parameter!FM2 coordinate@$\FM_2(\C)$ coordinate}
The variable $z$ in the spectral kernel $r(z)$ is the coordinate on
$\FM_2(\C)$ in the closed color of the Swiss-cheese correspondence:
the relative position $z_1 - z_2$ of two bulk insertion points in
the holomorphic plane, compactified by the Fulton--MacPherson
boundary. The pole at $z = 0$ is the collision divisor
$D_{\{1,2\}} \subset \FM_2(\C)$; its residue is the leading OPE
coefficient. The variable $z$ is the chiral analog of the
$q$-parameter in the Hecke algebra: both parametrize a
one-dimensional family of algebras interpolating between two
boundary conditions (the two strata of the compactified
configuration space), and both degenerate at $z = 0$ (resp.\
$q = 1$) where the two points (resp.\ the two Borel orbits)
collide.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Boundary flips as morphisms of Koszul dual modules}
\label{subsec:boundary-flips}
\index{flip operation|textbf}
\index{Koszul duality!boundary flips}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

In the CDG framework \cite{CDG2023}, \S4.3, a \emph{flip} is the operation
of coupling a two-dimensional boundary degree of freedom that changes the
boundary condition type, typically exchanging Neumann and Dirichlet
conditions for a given field.

\begin{definition}[Flip as $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module morphism]
\label{def:boundary-flip}
Let $M_N$ and $M_D$ denote the $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules corresponding to
Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions respectively (via
Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules}). A \emph{boundary flip} for a field
$\varphi$ is a chain map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flip-morphism}
 \mathrm{Flip}_\varphi \;:\; M_N \;\longrightarrow\; M_D
\end{equation}
in the homotopy category of $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules, induced by coupling
the boundary to a $2$d auxiliary system that absorbs the field $\varphi$
and replaces it by its conjugate.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Flip as natural transformation]
\label{prop:flip-natural-transformation}
\textup{(Costello--Dimofte--Gaiotto \cite{CDG2023}, \S4.3.)}
The flip functor acts on the line category
$\cC_T \simeq \mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}$ as a natural
transformation between the Neumann and Dirichlet module functors.
The induced map on $K$-theory exchanges the corresponding classes of
boundary conditions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Free chiral flip]
\label{ex:free-chiral-flip}
For the free chiral multiplet with fields $(\phi, \psi)$, flipping
$\phi \leftrightarrow \psi$ exchanges the vortex line families:
$V_{N > 0} \leftrightarrow V_{N < 0}$, where $V_N$ denotes the vortex
line of charge~$N$. In the $\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module language, this is the
exchange of the positive-charge and negative-charge simple modules.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Bar-cobar interpretation of flips]
\label{rem:flip-bar-cobar}
At the chain level, flips are chain maps between bar complex models:
the Neumann bar complex $\bar{B}(\cA_N)$ and the Dirichlet bar
complex $\bar{B}(\cA_D)$. The flip morphism~\eqref{eq:flip-morphism}
is a morphism in the homotopy category of
$\mathcal{A}^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-modules; its existence follows from
the Quillen equivalence (Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy-Koszul}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Vortex lines as the prototype]
\label{rem:vortex-line-prototype}
\index{vortex line!prototypical example}
The free chiral vortex lines $\{V_N\}_{N \in \mathbb{Z}}$ of the
$\beta\gamma$ system, analyzed in the beta--gamma chapter of
Volume~I, are the basic non-trivial line operators. Their
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}$-module structure, explicit $r$-matrix, and
fusion rules are computed there. The flip exchanging
$V_{N>0} \leftrightarrow V_{N<0}$ of
Example~\ref{ex:free-chiral-flip} is the abelian prototype; the
general flip of Definition~\ref{def:boundary-flip} reduces to the
$\beta\gamma$ case at gauge group $\mathrm{U}(1)$ with free
boundary.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Composite flips for gauge theories]
\label{ex:composite-flips}
\index{flip operation!composite}
For a gauge theory with multiple matter fields $\varphi_1, \ldots,
\varphi_r$, flipping a subset $S \subset \{1,\ldots,r\}$ produces a
composite morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite-flip}
 \mathrm{Flip}_S
 \;=\;
 \prod_{i \in S} \mathrm{Flip}_{\varphi_i}
 \;:\;
 M_{N}^{(S^c)} \otimes M_{D}^{(S)}
 \;\longrightarrow\;
 M_{D}^{(S^c)} \otimes M_{N}^{(S)},
\end{equation}
where the Neumann and Dirichlet modules for each field are exchanged
independently. The associativity
$\mathrm{Flip}_{S_1} \circ \mathrm{Flip}_{S_2} =
\mathrm{Flip}_{S_1 \triangle S_2}$ (symmetric difference) makes the
flips into a $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$-action on the boundary condition
space \cite{CDG2023}, \S4.3. This $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$-symmetry is
the bar-cobar manifestation of the group of boundary condition
changes. In the Chriss--Ginzburg framework, each flip is a
gauge transformation in $\operatorname{MC}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{\cA})$:
the MC element $\Theta_{\cA}$ is invariant under the full
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$ but the cross-polarization
(which side is $\cA$, which is $\cA^!$) is exchanged.
\end{example}

\section{Line-operator categories for standard families}
\label{sec:line-operator-standard-families}
\index{line operators!standard families|textbf}

\subsection{Heisenberg: free lines}
\label{subsec:line-op-heisenberg}

For $\cA=\cH_k$: the chiral Koszul dual is
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{ch}}=\Sym^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*)$, while the
open-colour dual is
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}=Y(\mathfrak{u}(1))$
(whose mode algebra $U(\mathfrak{u}(1)[t]) \cong \Bbbk[t]$
coincides with the mode algebra of~$\cH_{-k}$,
but is distinct as a chiral algebra),
$r_{\cH}(z)=k\Omega_{\cH}/z$, and
$\Theta_{\cH_k}=k\cdot\eta\otimes\Lambda$.
The $r$-matrix is rank-one tensor-valued; $k/z$ is its coefficient
after choosing the generator line.

\begin{computation}[Line-operator category for Heisenberg]
\label{comp:line-op-heisenberg}
\index{Heisenberg algebra!line operators}
$\cC_{\mathrm{line}}(\cH_k)
\simeq\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}
\simeq Y(\mathfrak{u}(1))\text{-}\mathbf{mod}
\simeq\cH_{-k}\text{-}\mathbf{mod}
\simeq\mathrm{Vect}^{\mathbb Z}$:
the category of $\mathbb Z$-graded vector spaces. The
grading is by charge $q\in\mathbb Z$; the Fock module
$\mathcal F_q$ of charge~$q$ is an irreducible
$\cH_{-k}$-module. The tensor product is
$\mathcal F_{q_1}\otimes\mathcal F_{q_2}
=\mathcal F_{q_1+q_2}$
(charge addition). The $r$-matrix acts on
$\mathcal F_{q_1}\otimes\mathcal F_{q_2}$ by the scalar
$k\,q_1\,q_2/z$: the braiding is
\[
R_{q_1,q_2}(z)
\;=\;
z^{k\,q_1\,q_2}\cdot\mathrm{id}
\;\in\;
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal F_{q_1}\otimes\mathcal F_{q_2}).
\]
The CYBE $[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0$
reduces to $0=0$ (the $r$-matrix is central).
No nontrivial line operators beyond Fock modules.
\end{computation}

\subsection{Affine Kac--Moody: Yangian modules and evaluation sector}
\label{subsec:line-op-affine}

For $\cA=\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_k$:
the chiral Koszul dual is
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{ch}}=\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_{-k-4}$,
while on the chirally Koszul affine locus the open-colour dual is
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}}=\Ydg(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. Its classical
spectral kernel is
$r(z)=k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z=k\,(e\otimes f+f\otimes e+\tfrac12 h\otimes h)/z$,
and on evaluation modules
$q=e^{i\pi/(k+2)}$.

\begin{computation}[Line-operator category for
$\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$]
\label{comp:line-op-sl2}
\index{Kac--Moody!line operators}
On the chirally Koszul affine locus,
\[
\cC_{\mathrm{line}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_k)
\simeq \Ydg(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\text{-}\mathbf{mod}.
\]
Restricting to evaluation modules and passing to the reduced braided
sector,
\[
\cC_{\mathrm{line}}^{\mathrm{red}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_k)
\big|_{\mathrm{eval}}
\simeq \operatorname{Rep}_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2),
\qquad q=e^{i\pi/(k+2)}.
\]
In this reduced evaluation sector, the simple objects are the usual
quantum-group simples $V_\lambda$ ($\lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}$,
with $0\le \lambda\le k$ in the integrable level-$k$ sector).

The $r$-matrix $r(z)=k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$ acts on
$V_{\lambda_1}\otimes V_{\lambda_2}$ via the Casimir:
\[
R_{\lambda_1,\lambda_2}(z)
\;=\;
\exp\Bigl(\frac{k\,\Omega_{\lambda_1,\lambda_2}}{z}\Bigr)
\;\in\;
\operatorname{End}(V_{\lambda_1}\otimes V_{\lambda_2})
[\![z^{-1}]\!].
\]
At $k=0$ this braiding collapses to the identity, as the
classical residue $r(z)=k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$ vanishes.
For $k=1$ (simplest non-abelian level): two simple objects
$V_0$ (trivial) and $V_1$ (fundamental). Fusion:
$V_1\otimes V_1=V_0$. The $R$-matrix at $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=1$
is a $4\times 4$ matrix with eigenvalue $q=e^{i\pi/3}$.

The KZ connection
$\nabla_{0,n}=d-\frac{1}{k+2}\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\,
d\log(z_i-z_j)$
is, on the affine Kac--Moody comparison surface, the flat
connection on conformal blocks for evaluation modules; its
monodromy representation is compared by the affine
Drinfeld--Kohno package with the braiding on the reduced
evaluation sector.

At $k=-2$ (critical level): $q=e^{i\pi/(k+2)}$ is undefined
(division by zero), and the evaluation-sector quantum-group
description degenerates. The KZ connection acquires a pole,
so this picture lies outside the chirally Koszul affine locus.
\end{computation}

\begin{computation}[Explicit $R$-matrix for
$\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ on $V\otimes V$]
\label{comp:explicit-r-matrix-sl2}
\index{R-matrix!explicit matrix entries!sl2@$\mathfrak{sl}_2$}
Let $V=\mathbb C^2$ (the fundamental representation).
The Casimir acts on $V\otimes V$ as
$\Omega=e\otimes f+f\otimes e+\tfrac12 h\otimes h$,
a $4\times 4$ matrix in the basis $\{v_+\otimes v_+,\,
v_+\otimes v_-,\,v_-\otimes v_+,\,v_-\otimes v_-\}$:
\[
\Omega
\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -1/2 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1/2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The $R$-matrix at level~$k$ is
$R(z)=1+k\,\Omega/(z(k{+}2))+O(z^{-2})$. At leading order:
\[
R(z)\big|_{k=1}
\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\frac{1}{6z} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\[3pt]
0 & 1-\frac{1}{6z} & \frac{1}{3z} & 0 \\[3pt]
0 & \frac{1}{3z} & 1-\frac{1}{6z} & 0 \\[3pt]
0 & 0 & 0 & 1+\frac{1}{6z}
\end{pmatrix}
+O(z^{-2}).
\]
Eigenvalues of $\Omega$: $+1/2$ (multiplicity~$3$, symmetric
part) and $-3/2$ (multiplicity~$1$, antisymmetric part).
The $R$-matrix decomposes:
$R(z)=(1+k/(2z(k{+}2)))\,P_{\mathrm{sym}}
+(1-3k/(2z(k{+}2)))\,P_{\mathrm{anti}}$,
where $P_{\mathrm{sym}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{anti}}$ are the
projectors onto the symmetric and antisymmetric parts
of $V\otimes V$.

At $z\to 0$: $R(z)\to\infty$ (pole). The residue
$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}R(z)=k\,\Omega/(k{+}2)$ is the
KZ connection coefficient.
\end{computation}

\subsection{Virasoro: Virasoro modules}
\label{subsec:line-op-virasoro}

For $\cA=\mathrm{Vir}_c$: the completed chiral Koszul-dual branch is
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{ch},\infty}$, represented by
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$ only on the generic strict Virasoro comparison
surface of Vol.~I
\texttt{landscape\_census.tex}, Proposition
\texttt{prop:virasoro-generic-koszul-dual}.
On the completed boundary-linear Virasoro comparison locus, the
line side is therefore modeled by modules for this strict
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$ representative.
(Unlike the affine lineage, the Virasoro does not admit a
separate Yangian reduction; see
Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:yangian-virasoro-nonformality}}.)
The Laplace kernel is
$r^L_c(z)=(\partial T \otimes \mathbf{1})/z+2(T \otimes \mathbf{1})/z^2+c\,(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})/(2z^4)$
(schematic: $\partial T/z+2T/z^2+c/(2z^4)$);
the collision residue is
$r^{\mathrm{coll}}_c(z) = (c/2)/z^3\,\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}
+ 2T\otimes\mathbf{1}/z$
(pole orders one lower, as in Volume~I's discussion of the three
related kernels).

\begin{theorem}[Virasoro comparison locus]
\label{comp:line-op-virasoro}
\index{Virasoro algebra!line operators}
Let $\cA=\mathrm{Vir}_c$ satisfy the generic Virasoro Koszul-duality
hypotheses of Vol.~I \texttt{landscape\_census.tex}, Proposition
\texttt{prop:virasoro-generic-koszul-dual},
and let the boundary vacuum lie in the completed boundary-linear exact
sector of Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules}. Then the completed
Virasoro comparison line category is modeled by
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}\text{-mod}^{\mathrm{dg}}$:
the dg category of modules for the dual-central-charge
Virasoro algebra. In the highest-weight sector, the standard objects
are the Verma modules $M(h',26-c)$ and the generic simple objects are
their irreducible quotients $L(h',26-c)$.

The Laplace kernel $r^L_c(z)=\partial T/z+2T/z^2+c/(2z^4)$ has a
fourth-order pole (the collision residue $r^{\mathrm{coll}}_c(z) =
(c/2)/z^3 + 2T/z$ has a cubic pole). The standard Yangian
$r$-matrix has a simple pole; the higher-order poles here reflect
the conformal anomaly. The Virasoro algebra is a central extension
of the Witt algebra rather than a Lie algebra with invariant inner
product, and its cocycle contributes the anomalous poles.

The genus-$0$ shadow connection gives the
Virasoro conformal Ward connection for $n$-point conformal blocks:
\[
\nabla^{\mathrm{hol}}_{0,n}(\Phi)
\;=\;
\Bigl(
\partial_{z_i}
-\sum_{j\neq i}
\Bigl(\frac{h_j}{(z_i-z_j)^2}
+\frac{\partial_{z_j}}{z_i-z_j}\Bigr)
\Bigr)\Phi
\;=\;0.
\]
On the degenerate-module comparison surface, the corresponding
null-vector constraints then specialize this Ward system to the
level-$2$ BPZ equations; the higher-level BPZ systems arise from
higher-depth collision residues of~$\Theta_c$.

At $c=26$, the Virasoro comparison lands on
$\mathrm{Vir}_0$. Here $\kappa=0$, and the shadow
connection is flat at leading order. On this comparison locus,
the line category is modeled by $\mathrm{Vir}_0\text{-mod}$,
the ``pure gravity'' sector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Vol.~I identifies the generic Virasoro Verdier/Koszul partner as
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:lines_as_modules} then applies
on the completed boundary-linear exact locus and gives the module
category for the open-colour Koszul dual. The Ward connection is the
standard action of the Virasoro modes on conformal blocks after
collision-residue extraction; null-vector quotients impose the BPZ
relations in the usual degenerate highest-weight sectors. The
$c=26$ specialization is the substitution $c'=26-c=0$:
the original side has $\kappaChHodge(\mathrm{Vir}_{26})=13$, while the dual
side has central charge $0$ and
$\kappaChHodge(\mathrm{Vir}_{0})=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The RTT presentation and mode structure}
\label{subsubsec:virasoro-rtt}

The collision residue $r^{\mathrm{coll}}_c(z) = (c/2)/z^3 + 2T/z$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:field-theory-r}) has a cubic pole, in
contrast with the simple pole of the affine RTT relation. The cubic
pole produces three mode strata: the affine $r$-matrix
$r(z) = k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$ has a single residue $k\,\Omega_0$; the
Virasoro collision residue has residues $\Omega_0$, $\Omega_1$,
$\Omega_2$ at $z^{-1}$, $z^{-2}$, $z^{-3}$, with the full series
$r(z) = \sum_{m \ge 0} \Omega_m/z^{m+1}$ continuing to all orders
(Construction~\ref{constr:thqg-V-vir-r-explicit}).

\begin{construction}[Virasoro RTT comparison relation]
\label{constr:virasoro-rtt-relation}
\index{Virasoro algebra!RTT relation|textbf}
\index{RTT relation!Virasoro}
On the Virasoro comparison locus of
Theorem~\ref{comp:line-op-virasoro}, the quantum $R$-matrix
$R(z)$ on the modeled line category is the
monodromy of the ordered bar connection
(Construction~\ref{constr:r-matrix-monodromy}).
Define the transfer matrix
$\mathcal{T}(z) \in
\operatorname{End}(V) \otimes
\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}[\![z^{-1}]\!]$
for any $\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$-module $V$ by the generating function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-transfer}
\mathcal{T}(z)
\;=\;
\sum_{n \ge 0}
z^{-n-1}\,
\pi_V(L_n),
\end{equation}
where $\pi_V \colon \mathrm{Vir}_{26-c} \to \operatorname{End}(V)$
is the representation map and $\{L_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}$ are the
Virasoro generators at dual central charge $c' = 26 - c$.

The RTT relation takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-rtt}
\boxed{
R_{12}(z{-}w)\,
\mathcal{T}_1(z)\,
\mathcal{T}_2(w)
\;=\;
\mathcal{T}_2(w)\,
\mathcal{T}_1(z)\,
R_{12}(z{-}w),
}
\end{equation}
where the subscripts denote tensor factors and
$R_{12}(z)$ is the spectral $R$-matrix of
Corollary~\textup{\ref{cor:gravity-spectral-r}}.
The classical limit $\hbar \to 0$ (equivalently
$c \to \infty$, the semiclassical gravity limit) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-rtt-classical}
[\mathcal{T}_1(z),\, \mathcal{T}_2(w)]
\;=\;
\frac{1}{c}\bigl[r_{12}(z{-}w),\,
\mathcal{T}_1(z)\,\mathcal{T}_2(w)\bigr]
\;+\; O(c^{-2}),
\end{equation}
with $r_{12}(z) = (c/2)/z^3 + 2T/z$ the classical
collision residue.
\end{construction}

\begin{remark}[Derivation on the Virasoro comparison locus]
The RTT relation~\eqref{eq:virasoro-rtt} is the specialisation
of the general RTT framework

\end{remark}

\begin{computation}[Mode decomposition of the Virasoro RTT

\label{comp:virasoro-rtt-modes}
\index{Virasoro algebra!RTT mode relations}

\end{computation}

\begin{proposition}[Ponsot--Teschner comparison obstruction for Virasoro evaluation modules]
\label{comp:virasoro-eval-fusion}
\index{Virasoro algebra!fusion of evaluation modules}
\index{fusion rules!Virasoro evaluation modules}

Virasoro comparison locus defined by the ordered-bar monodromy, and
let $\mathfrak B_{\mathrm{PT}}$ be the Ponsot--Teschner fusion-kernel
braiding on the corresponding highest-weight Virasoro block category
with the same choice of contour and normalization. Their discrepancy
is the obstruction class
\[
 o_{\mathrm{PT}}
 \in

 \operatorname{Cone}\bigl(
 \operatorname{Def}(\mathfrak B_{\mathrm{bar}})
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Def}(\mathfrak B_{\mathrm{PT}})
 \bigr)[1]\right).
\]

tensor product of two evaluation modules at spectral points
$z_1,z_2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-fusion}
\operatorname{ev}_{z_1}(M_{h_1})
\;\otimes_{R(z_{12})}\;
\operatorname{ev}_{z_2}(M_{h_2})
\;=\;
\int_{\mathcal{C}} M_{h}\,
d\mu_{h_1,h_2}(h),
\end{equation}
where $z_{12} = z_1 - z_2$, the integral is over the
Plancherel contour $\mathcal{C} = Q'/2 + i\mathbb{R}_+$
with $Q' = b' + b'^{-1}$ and $c' = 1 + 6Q'^2$,
and $d\mu_{h_1,h_2}$ is the Virasoro Plancherel measure
determined by the OPE coefficients.

The corresponding $R$-matrix braiding on the tensor product is
(Corollary~\ref{cor:gravity-spectral-r}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-braiding-eval}
R(z_{12})\big|_{M_{h_1} \otimes M_{h_2}}
\;=\;
\bigoplus_{h}
e^{2\pi i(h - h_1 - h_2)}\,
\mathrm{id}_{M_h},
\end{equation}
diagonal in the fusion-channel basis with eigenvalues
that are Aharonov--Bohm phases. This is the Virasoro
counterpart of~\eqref{eq:r-matrix-diagonal} on the vanishing locus
of~$o_{\mathrm{PT}}$.

\emph{Rational central charge (minimal models).}\enspace
For $c' = 1 - 6(p'-q')^2/(p'q')$ with
$\gcd(p',q') = 1$, the tensor product is a finite direct
sum: $M_{h_1} \otimes M_{h_2} = \bigoplus_{h \in
\mathrm{Kac}(p',q')} N^{h}_{h_1 h_2}\, L(h, c')$.
The comparison line category truncates to a finite ribbon
category, namely the modular tensor category of the
$(p',q')$ minimal model at central charge $c'$.

\emph{Self-dual point $c = 13$.}\enspace
At $c = 13$: $c' = 13$, and
$\mathrm{Vir}_{13}^! = \mathrm{Vir}_{13}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gravity-koszul-dual}).

$\mathrm{Vir}_{13}\text{-mod}$ is self-dual under
the Koszul involution; the comparison between the braiding and its
opposite is the involutive specialization of~$o_{\mathrm{PT}}$ at
$c=13$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both $\mathfrak B_{\mathrm{bar}}$ and $\mathfrak B_{\mathrm{PT}}$ are
braided tensor functors on the same highest-weight block surface after
fixing contour and normalization data. The deformation complex of a
braided tensor functor controls its first obstruction in degree two;
therefore the cone of the comparison map carries the obstruction to
identifying the two braidings. Vanishing supplies the comparison
isomorphism, and the displayed direct-integral formula is precisely
the Ponsot--Teschner decomposition transported through that
isomorphism. The rational and self-dual specializations are obtained
by substituting the minimal-model contour and $c'=13$, respectively.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Why the Virasoro has no separate Yangian}
\label{subsubsec:virasoro-no-separate-yangian}

\begin{proposition}[Virasoro: the line algebra and $U(\mathrm{Witt}{[t]})$]
\label{prop:virasoro-no-yangian-deformation}
\index{Virasoro algebra!absence of Yangian deformation|textbf}
\index{Yangian!does not exist for Virasoro}
On the Virasoro comparison locus, the strict open-colour
same-family representative
$\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}} = \mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$ is not a filtered
deformation of $U(\mathrm{Witt}{[t]})$ in the sense of a standard
Yangian. The obstruction is twofold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{Central extension.}\; The Virasoro algebra is a
 non-trivial central extension of the Witt algebra
 $\mathrm{Witt} = \mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}])$ by
 $\mathbb{C}$. The extension class
 $[\omega] \in H^2(\mathrm{Witt}, \mathbb{C})$ is the
 Gel'fand--Fuchs cocycle
 $\omega(L_m, L_n) = \tfrac{1}{12}(m^3 - m)\,\delta_{m+n,0}$.
 The classical limit of a standard Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{g})$ is
 $U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]})$, the loop algebra of a finite-dimensional
 Lie algebra; the Virasoro algebra is infinite-dimensional, and
 the Gel'fand--Fuchs cocycle has no finite-dimensional analogue.
\item \emph{Non-formality.}\; The $A_\infty$ structure on
 $\mathrm{Vir}_c$ has $m_3\ne0$ and $m_4\ne0$ generically, with
 all-degree non-truncation governed by the KZ analytic SDR and
 wheel-residue survival hypotheses of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:vir-truncation}. The associated graded
 $U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]})$ of a Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{g})$ is formal
 ($m_k = 0$ for $k \ge 3$) and the deformation lives in
 $\HH^2(U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]}))$
 (Proposition~\ref{prop:yangian-deformation-obstruction}). The
 non-vanishing of the Virasoro higher $A_\infty$ operations
 entangles the Yangian and Lie-algebra directions of the
 deformation problem.
\end{enumerate}
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}$ is simultaneously the boundary algebra, the
Koszul dual algebra, and the line-side algebra; the infinite tower
of higher $A_\infty$ operations $\{m_k\}_{k \ge 3}$ is the Virasoro
algebra structure itself.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a): the space of central extensions
$H^2(\mathrm{Witt}, \mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}$ is one-dimensional,
generated by the Gel'fand--Fuchs cocycle. A Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak{g})$ is a deformation of $U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]})$ in the
Harrison cohomology class $\Har^2(U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]}))$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:yangian-deformation-obstruction}). For
$\mathfrak{g}$ infinite-dimensional, the Harrison cohomology of
$U(\mathfrak{g}{[t]})$ falls outside the K\"unneth formula of
Proposition~\ref{prop:HH-Ugt-decomposition}, and the leading
vanishing lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:harrison-leading-vanishing}) no
longer applies. The Virasoro Harrison obstruction group
$\Har^3(U(\mathrm{Witt}{[t]}))$ admits non-vanishing classes.

Part~(b): non-formality begins with $m_3 \ne 0$ and $m_4\ne0$,
proved in the gravity chapter (Section~\ref{subsec:gravity-ainf}):
the quartic OPE pole forces a non-zero associator $A_3 \ne 0$, which
forces $m_3 \ne 0$, and the quartic contact invariant
$\mathfrak{Q} = 10/[c(5c+22)] \ne 0$ (for generic $c$) forces
$m_4 \ne 0$. All-degree non-truncation of the shadow tower is
conditional on the KZ analytic SDR and wheel-residue survival
hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:vir-truncation}. For formal
algebras, the Yangian deformation class lives in a separate
cohomological direction from the Lie bracket; for non-formal
algebras, these directions are entangled.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Virasoro Steinberg obstruction]

 $\mathrm{Vir}^* \simeq

 $H^0_{\mathrm{DS}}(M) = H^0(M, Q_{\mathrm{DS}})$.

\end{enumerate}

\[
 o_{\mathrm{St,Vir}}\in
 H^2\!\left(
 \operatorname{Cone}\bigl(
 \operatorname{Conv}_{\mathrm{St}}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)
 \xrightarrow{\,H^\bullet_{\mathrm{DS}}\,}
 \operatorname{Conv}_{\mathrm{Vir}}
 \bigr)[1]\right).
\]
Vanishing of $o_{\mathrm{St,Vir}}$ supplies a reduced Virasoro
Steinberg convolution algebra acting on
$\mathrm{Vir}_{26-c}\text{-mod}$; non-vanishing is the obstruction to
the geometric realization.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spectral $R$-matrix: holomorphic vs topological dichotomy}
\label{subsec:line-operators-hol-top-dichotomy}

\begin{remark}[The two faces of the $R$-matrix]
\label{rem:line-operators-hol-top-dichotomy}
A line operator on the $3$d HT slab has two natural exchange data,
which must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item the \emph{holomorphic} (chiral) braiding
$R^{\mathrm{hol}}(z) \in (\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}})^{\otimes 2}((z))$,
where $z$ is the spectral parameter measuring the holomorphic
displacement of the two line operators along the curve $X\subset
X\times\R$; this is the open-colour shadow of the
$\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$-bar coproduct and lives in the closed
colour of $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$;
\item the \emph{topological} ordering swap
$\sigma^{\mathrm{top}} \in \Sigma_2$ acting on
$\mathrm{Mod}(\cA^!_{\mathrm{line}})^{\otimes 2}$ by exchanging the
two line operators along the topological direction $\R$; this is
the open-colour braided structure on the line category, lifted from
the deconcatenation coproduct on the bar coalgebra.
\end{enumerate}
At a generic level (non-critical, $\Psi$ not a positive rational),
the spectral braiding admits the standard KZ comparison after fixing
normalisation, contour, and channel data. It does not become the
topological swap by setting the holomorphic displacement to zero; the
topological swap is a separate ordering operation. At the critical
level $k=-h^\vee$ the KZ normalisation
$\Omega/((k+h^\vee)z)$ leaves its domain, while the trace-form kernel
$-h^\vee\Omega/z$ is a finite simple-pole expression. The
Sugawara construction collapses and the Feigin--Frenkel centre/opers
replace the non-critical KZ comparison.
\end{remark}

\begin{convention}[Level-prefix discipline for line-operator spectral
$R$-matrices]
\label{conv:line-operators-rmatrix-level-prefix}
Every line-operator spectral $R$-matrix in this chapter carries an
explicit level prefix and is checked at $k=0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Affine Kac--Moody at level $k$, trace-form: $r(z) = k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$;
$k=0$ kills this singular central trace-form residue only. For
non-abelian $\fg$ the simple-pole bracket remains as the regular
$z^0$ Lie part, so this is not a symmetric line-category collapse.
\item Affine Kac--Moody, KZ normalisation: $r(z) = \Omega/((k+h^\vee)z)$;
$k=0$ gives $\Omega/(h^\vee z)\ne 0$ for non-abelian $\fg$ (Lie
bracket persists in line-operator OPE).
\item Critical level $k=-h^\vee$: KZ normalisation diverges; trace-form
gives $r(z) = -h^\vee \Omega/z$, finite. The Sugawara construction
collapses, and line operators carry the Feigin--Frenkel-centre
shadow.
\item Heisenberg,
$r^{\mathrm{Heis}}(z)=k\Omega_{\cH}/z$ with
$\Omega_{\cH}=J\otimes J$; abelian, rank-one single-pole coefficient
$k/z$.
\item Virasoro,
\(r_{\mathrm{Vir}}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z) = (c/2)/z^3 + 2T/z\);
cubic+simple collision pole structure (the gravitational line-side seed).
\end{enumerate}
Every $\Omega/z$ appearance in this chapter and across Volumes
I--III carries its level prefix.
\end{convention}

\begin{theorem}[Strong/weak chiral-Yangian comparison obstruction \ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{thm:strong-weak-chiral-yangian-comparison-obstruction}
Let
\[
Y_{\mathrm{weak}}(A)
:=
(A,R(z),\otimes_z)
\]
denote the \(E_1\)-factorization-plus-RTT datum, with
\[
R_{12}(z-w)R_{13}(z)R_{23}(w)
=
R_{23}(w)R_{13}(z)R_{12}(z-w),
\]
and let
\[
Y_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{ch}}(A)
:=
\bigl(A,\Delta_z,R(z),S(z),\epsilon,\{m_k\}_{k\ge2},
\Theta_{\mathrm{mod}}\bigr)
\]
denote the modular-MC chiral-Yangian datum, with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strong-chiral-yangian-modular-mc}
d\Theta_{\mathrm{mod}}
+\frac12[\Theta_{\mathrm{mod}},\Theta_{\mathrm{mod}}]=0
\end{equation}
in the completed modular deformation dg Lie algebra.

An identification of \(Y_{\mathrm{weak}}(A)\) with
\(Y_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{ch}}(A)\) is licensed by a filtered
comparison morphism of deformation problems
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rttdef-to-modmc-comparison-map}
\Psi_{\mathrm{RTT}\to\mathrm{mod}}\colon
\operatorname{RTTDef}(A,R)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{MC}_{\mathrm{mod}}(A).
\end{equation}
Its tangent map must send the RTT infinitesimal deformation to the first
modular Taylor coefficient:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rttdef-tangent-to-theta-one}
d\Psi_{\mathrm{RTT}\to\mathrm{mod}}(\dot R(z))=\Theta_1.
\end{equation}
Writing
\(\Theta_{\mathrm{mod}}=\Theta_0+\Theta_1+\Theta_2+\cdots\), the
second obstruction to extending the RTT infinitesimal through the modular
MC tower is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strong-weak-chiral-yangian-ob2}
\operatorname{ob}_2(\dot R)
=
\left[
d_{\Theta_0}\Theta_2+\frac12[\Theta_1,\Theta_1]
\right]
\in H^2_{\mathrm{mod}}(A).
\end{equation}
Consequently the slogan ``monodromy equals the \(R\)-matrix'' is a
theorem only on the locus where the KZ/KZB monodromy functor lands in
\(Y_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{ch}}(A)\) through
\eqref{eq:rttdef-to-modmc-comparison-map} and the modular obstruction
classes vanish. Away from that locus it is only a statement about
\(Y_{\mathrm{weak}}(A)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weak datum is exactly the RTT deformation problem: first-order
deformations are infinitesimal changes of the \(R\)-matrix satisfying the
linearized Yang--Baxter equation. The strong datum is a Maurer--Cartan
problem in the completed modular deformation dg Lie algebra, so any
comparison identifying the two deformation theories must be a filtered
morphism of the corresponding formal moduli problems. Its linear part
sends first-order RTT deformations to first-order modular
Maurer--Cartan coefficients, which is
\eqref{eq:rttdef-tangent-to-theta-one}.

Expanding \eqref{eq:strong-chiral-yangian-modular-mc} around
\(\Theta_0\) gives at second order
\[
d_{\Theta_0}\Theta_2+\frac12[\Theta_1,\Theta_1].
\]
This expression is \(d_{\Theta_0}\)-closed by the Jacobi identity and the
first-order equation, hence defines the class
\eqref{eq:strong-weak-chiral-yangian-ob2}. Vanishing of this class is
exactly the condition that the first-order RTT deformation extend to
second order in the modular MC tower; higher orders are identical
coefficient extractions. The final assertion follows because KZ/KZB
monodromy supplies a strong chiral-Yangian statement only after it
factors through this modular MC comparison.
\end{proof}

\item \emph{Heterotic on $T^4$} (Harvey--Moore 1996): line operators

\end{itemize}
The primitive BKM denominator is $\Delta_5$, of weight~$5$. The scalar
branch uses three normalisations:
\[
 D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
 \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
 \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
Here $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}$ is the unnormalised DVV/DMVV Igusa
square, hence a genuine weight-$10$ Siegel modular form; it is the
scalar generating function counting all four frames in lockstep.
The symbol $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}$ denotes the
Oberdieck--Pandharipande/Oberdieck--Pixton reduced-DT monic product
convention. The reduced \(K3 \times E\) scalar branch imported from that
comparison is
\[
 Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{OP/DT}}
 =-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
 =-D_5^{-2}
 =-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The normalisation is fixed by the exact coefficient computation
\texttt{igusa-cusp-form/compute/verify\_square\_root.py}: coefficients
are expanded after the leading monomial of $64^{-1}\Delta_5$, with
$[qrs]\,(64^{-1}\Delta_5)/(qrs)^{1/2}=93$ and
$29-93=-64$, the $q\,r$ coefficient of $\phi_{0,1}$.
The constant \(4096=64^2\) is a normalisation conversion and
the minus sign is the OP/DT scalar convention; neither contains the
compact Hall orientation, the compact CoHA bracket, or the
Hall--Drinfeld double.
The double
$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ is the non-abelian chiral target only after
the compact finite-window double and the primitive recognition data
are supplied; finite Rees hCS--Hall models are filtered target
approximations of the denominator algebra.
The four duality frames determine the scalar branch and the physical
source; they do not prove the compact Hall--Drinfeld/Borcherds
recognition theorem.
